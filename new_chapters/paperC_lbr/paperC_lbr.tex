\title{Off-Catalogue Question Emission and Rendering\\
for Cold-Start Preference Elicitation}
\author{}
\date{September 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent A cold-start recommender that interviews its user can ask only what its question catalogue
contains. We show that the catalogue is the binding constraint and that it can be removed. At the
interview states we study, the nearest catalogue question is close to the policy's emitted direction
in the policy's own head space (cosine $0.93$), yet that question's own text is anti-aligned with the
direction implied by the measured gains, at $-0.158$ $[-0.224,\,-0.089]$, while an off-catalogue word
rendered at that state carries it at $+0.204$ $[+0.117,\,+0.291]$. We then construct the missing
renderer. A two-layer adapter into a frozen language model expresses such a direction in English, and
the vector conditions the resulting text: against a correspondence-scrambled twin the difference is
$+0.111$ $[+0.105,\,+0.117]$ through an independently fitted bridge, with the true arm at $107\%$ of
the gold phrase's own ceiling and source identification $0.678$ (chance $0.5$). Retrained on
question-shaped targets it emits well-formed questions $94.7\%$ of the time and the conditioning
survives; templating costs $23\%$ of the instrument's ceiling, a quantity the verbalisation literature
has not reported because it does not measure against a same-instrument ceiling. Closing the loop, a
synthesised direction is rendered, folded back, and costs $+0.018$ NDCG@10 in transport under a
declared-circular answer model shared by both arms. The claim is one of capability: a collaborative
latent, rendered as language, left unsnapped.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A cold-start recommender that interviews its user must select each question from a fixed list. In CASPER
(Cold-start Adaptive Selection for Preference Elicitation in Recommendation), the cold-start
conversational recommender studied here, the list holds $19{,}390$ entries, comprising $18{,}359$ films and $1{,}031$
concepts, and the interview problem reduces to an argmax over it: which entry separates the current
cohort, which entry a person can answer, and in which order to ask. That list is the action space.

We study interview states at which the direction implied by the measured gains is not represented in
the action space. Three questions into an interview, with no answer to \emph{do you like
better-than-expected films?}, no answer on \emph{True Lies}, and a dislike for \emph{Apollo 13}, the
policy computes a query direction and snaps it to the nearest catalogue entry, which it asks in place
of the computed direction. The quantity lost in that snap is measurable. At the interview states we
study, the nearest catalogue entry is close to the emitted direction in the policy's own head space,
at cosine $0.93$, which is as close as the catalogue reaches. Pushed through a bridge fitted
independently of every other component reported here, that entry's own gold phrase is anti-aligned
with the direction implied by the measured gains, at $-0.158$ $[-0.224,\,-0.089]$, while a word
produced at that state by a small language model reading the same direction carries it at $+0.204$
$[+0.117,\,+0.291]$: a gap of $+0.362$ with the interval excluding zero (\S\ref{sec:loop}, where two
caveats are attached to the magnitude). The direction is expressible in English, and the catalogue
does not contain a sentence that expresses it.

The remainder of the paper establishes the components required for this comparison and audits what
the renderer produces. \S\ref{sec:point} concerns the emitted point: a policy trained on gain-weighted
blends of the candidates whose value was measured at each state emits off-catalogue directions while
continuing to run the tree's ordinary interview. \S\ref{sec:english} trains an adapter into a frozen
\texttt{distilgpt2} and tests whether the generated text depends on the input vector, against a twin
trained identically on scrambled vector--text pairs, first on bare phrases and then on question-shaped
targets. \S\ref{sec:loop} closes the loop and prices the translation; \S\ref{sec:demo} reports a full
synthesised interview.

\paragraph{What is claimed, as a conjunction.} Each component has prior art and we claim no component
in isolation. Prior methods that use belief geometry to choose an elicitation question reduce the
covariance to a scalar and use it to score a finite pool; we take the argmax direction itself as the
emitted object, render it as language, and audit the result. The unoccupied conjunction is a
collaborative latent, rendered as language, left unsnapped, with the decoded point chosen by an
interview policy at an interview state, the renderer audited against a correspondence-scrambled
latent, and faithfulness scored causally by substituting the text where the latent was. The claim
concerns capability rather than value: a direction outside the bank can be emitted, expressed and
audited, and we never claim that asking it improves a recommendation.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Choosing a question from belief geometry: not ours.} The principal-axis rule (probe along
the leading eigenvector of the posterior covariance) is long established and overdetermined. Ji, Xue
and Carin state it verbatim, and state the synthesise-or-snap fork itself: if such flexibility is not
feasible, design a library of possible next projections~\cite{ji2008bcs}. Info-Synth reaches the same
direction from mutual information under an ideal-point probit~\cite{nadagouda2026infosynth}; Zhang et
al.\ adaptively minimise the trace of the posterior covariance of a respondent's own latent
factor~\cite{zhang2020activemf}; Eigentaste elicits on principal components of the rating
matrix~\cite{goldberg2001eigentaste}; Fonarev et al.\ select elicitation items by maximal
volume~\cite{fonarev2016rectangular}; the blend target we use as supervision is DGRL's
construction~\cite{hoppe2026dgrl}. What remains open is what happens after the argmax direction is
computed.\footnote{Loepp, Hussein and Ziegler (CHI 2014) reportedly elicits preferences by presenting
item sets at the extremes of each latent factor in turn. We could not obtain the paper through three
routes and therefore make no novelty claim about interrogating along a latent direction.}

\paragraph{Answerability: a thirty-four-year-old failure, and its two fixes.} Baum and Lang showed in
1992 that a learner which synthesises the maximally informative input and asks a human to label it
can perform poorly for a reason that better optimisation does not address: the informative point is a
fusion of two classes, a person cannot label it, and the query returns noise~\cite{baum1992query}.
Zhu and Bento reproduced the effect with GANs, reporting that labellers failed to confidently identify
most generated images~\cite{zhu2017gaal}. The two standard responses correspond to the two options
available here: snap to a real neighbour, or verbalise. Snapping is the majority answer, and within
recommendation Vendrov et al.\ implement it directly, optimising continuous hypothetical item vectors
and then projecting back to the nearest real items~\cite{vendrov2020gradient}; removing the snap is
the risk we take rather than a shortcut we decline. Where a design can be physically executed the
synthesised query does reach people, because there the world renders it: LineCoSpar proposes a gait
from no pool and has an exoskeleton walk it for six real subjects~\cite{tucker2020linecospar}, and
adaptive design optimisation does the same in psychophysics~\cite{ahn2020ado}. Recommendation has no
such renderer, which is why the field snapped to a catalogue, and why we propose that language is the
missing one.

\paragraph{Verbalising latents, and the validation gap.} ELM maps vectors from a frozen recommendation
model into a language model's token-embedding space and describes them, including synthesised
interpolations~\cite{tennenholtz2023elm}; we adopt that mechanism rather than introduce one. EAGLE
steers a frozen LLM toward high-utility regions of a MovieLens-25M behavioural embedding and has no
decoder: it edits text and encodes the result, so no latent point is ever mapped to language, and its
authors name the price, namely that coverage is limited by the action space~\cite{tennenholtz2024eagle}.
GeneRec claims off-corpus generation in the item channel~\cite{wang2023generec}, whereas ours is the
question channel; GenMQD generates a textual description of a wanted instance in vision active
learning~\cite{genmqd2024}. DeCap gives the measured argument for a soft snap over a hard one: CIDEr
$8.1$ for a raw off-manifold vector, $27.1$ for a hard nearest-neighbour snap, and $42.1$ for a
softmax-weighted blend~\cite{li2023decap}. Round-trip cosine alone is not evidence, since zero-shot
inverters reach cosine above $90$ at F1 only above $50$~\cite{zhang2025zsinvert}. The validation gap
is one control, stated narrowly: we are aware of no embedding-verbalisation system that reports what
its renderer produces from a correspondence-scrambled latent. The construction is not ours to claim.
Heap et al.\ found automated interpretability scores higher for randomly initialised transformers
($0.87$ against a trained $0.79$), while the only null that separated, at chance $0.50$, scrambled the
token-embedding correspondence~\cite{heap2026autointerp}, and Tang et al.\ permute voxel responses to
destroy response-to-stimulus correspondence while preserving response
statistics~\cite{tang2023semantic}. Its application to a rendered elicitation question, where no
paired reference text exists, is ours. The metric we import is Hewitt et al.'s causal one: substitute
the text back where the latent was and measure how much of the latent's effect it
reproduces~\cite{hewitt2025neologism}.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

Nothing in CASPER is refitted. The recommender checkpoint, the fold-in operator and the
question bank are frozen certified artifacts; the bank holds $19{,}390$ questions in two channels
($18{,}359$ items, $1{,}031$ concepts); the ruler is full and tail NDCG@10 on the canonical split's
$10{,}000$ validation users, and the $10{,}000$ canonical test users were not touched. The base policy
reads the interview state, emits a continuous query vector $\zhat$, and asks whichever bank question
scores highest against it under $s(q\mid\text{state}) = \langle u(h),k(\varphi_q)\rangle +
b(\varphi_q)$; we measure in homogeneous coordinates so that $\langle\zhat,e_q\rangle$ reproduces $s$
exactly. Faithfulness is measured through a \emph{tower bridge}, a text-to-collaborative map fitted
independently of both the emitter and the renderer, so that no reading is closed over CASPER's own
components. Three conventions bind every number below: every statistic carries an interval and
declares its aggregation unit; every headline number is printed beside its trivial floor (state-blind
constant, random direction, prior-only, or chance); and every registered reading rule was applied to
its own null at design time.

\paragraph{The circularity contract.} The simulated answerer places the policy at interview states and
performs no other function, with one declared exception: the translation-cost measurement of
\S\ref{sec:cost} requires an answer model by construction. There we follow the field's practice,
including Info-Synth's geometry-dependent response model~\cite{nadagouda2026infosynth} and PEBOL's
simulated respondents~\cite{austin2024bayesian}, declare the circularity in our own voice, place both
arms under the same simulator, and read only the difference. Absolute rows are printed and labelled;
no version of the claim that asking the synthesised question improves recommendations appears here.

\section{The emitted point}
\label{sec:point}

The deployed policy's emitted vector never lies on the question it selects: over all $160{,}000$
validation states the maximum cosine to the snapped entry is $0.1203$. That falsification is
unconditional, but the raw geometry is dominated by a common mode. Cross-entropy constrains only score
differences, so the head is free to place a large shared offset in $k(\varphi)$, and it does (median
top score $-82.7$; the bank's mean pairwise cosine is $0.964$ raw against $0.0018$ centred). With the
mean removed, the picture becomes informative: the emission's cosine to the entry it selects has
median $+0.488$ against $+0.170$ for a norm-matched random direction. The trained emission is
therefore a large common-mode offset plus a genuine bank-aligned direction, but it is a scoring
functional rather than a target in question space. To obtain a target we retrain, replacing the
one-hot distillation target with a gain-weighted blend of the top-$m$ candidates whose value was
measured at that state, so that the target is off-catalogue by construction while every gradient still
comes from a question we measured, and training jointly with a replay cross-entropy toward the tree's
ordinary picks so that the object remains a policy. Both behaviours are retained by the single object:
the retrained policy runs the tree's ordinary interview essentially unchanged (Table~\ref{tab:point})
and emits blends whose median top-1 ingredient weight is $0.2254$, with $0$ of $72$ states above
$0.5$, against a random floor of $0.121$.

\section{Rendering the direction in English}
\label{sec:english}

The renderer is a two-layer MLP producing eight soft prefix tokens into a frozen \texttt{distilgpt2},
trained on the $19{,}390$ bank entries with a $20\%$ identity-held-out split stratified by channel and
popularity decile. The load-bearing control is a twin trained identically on correspondence-scrambled
pairs: same architecture, data and optimiser, with a channel-preserving permutation of which vector
goes with which text. This is the only renderer variant under which the statement that the vector
conditions what is said is literally true, and therefore the only one in which scrambling tests
rendering rather than retrieval. The arms separate by epoch~2 in both runs.

\paragraph{The mechanism, on bare phrases.} On $3{,}670$ held-out item questions, through the
independent bridge, the true adapter's text re-encodes toward its own held-out vector at $+0.1114$
$[+0.1054,\,+0.1173]$ above the scrambled twin, with the true arm at $107\%$ of what the gold phrase
itself earns through the same instrument, and it identifies its source vector against a same-channel
decoy at $0.6778$ $[0.6631,\,0.6931]$, above the gold phrase's own $0.6624$. The text generated from a
vector therefore depends on that vector, on held-out questions, through an independently fitted
bridge, per channel, with intervals.

\paragraph{The effect survives conversion to askable form.} An interview asks questions, so we retrain
the same adapter on question-shaped targets built from the same bank entries with a small per-channel
template family, one template sampled per example per epoch. Everything else is held fixed, including
the scrambled twin. Under a rule fixed in advance, $94.7\%$ $[0.9398,\,0.9542]$ of held-out
generations are well-formed questions, and every registered reading passes under both decodings and on
both channels (Table~\ref{tab:english}).

\paragraph{Naturalness costs verifiability, and the cost can be priced.} The gate is calibrated
against what the true text earns through the same instrument, so a change of output format is a change
of instrument and the ceiling must be re-measured. The ceiling moves substantially. Wrapping a phrase
in question boilerplate lowers what a perfect rendering can score by $23\%$ on items
($0.08920\to0.06828$) and $17\%$ on concepts, because the words that make a question askable are the
same words for every question in the channel and therefore add surface carrying no information about
the direction. For this reason the bare-phrase adapter's $107\%$ of ceiling and the question adapter's
$71\%$ are not two readings of one number. Reporting a figure of this kind requires a same-instrument
ceiling, that is, a measurement of what the ground-truth text itself earns through the identical
chain, which is exactly the calibration the verbalisation literature does not run. Hence the rule:
re-measure the ceiling before reading any gate under a changed output format.

\paragraph{Machine-only synonyms, and the limits of a plausibility panel.} Content preservation is
effectively zero, with exact content match $0.0003$ and token-F1 $0.055$, and the vector is carried
nonetheless, at $61\%$ identification against chance $50\%$ and a ceiling of $0.6316$. The failure
mode is legible in the outputs: \emph{The Shawshank Redemption} renders as ``Have you seen Pulp
Fiction (1995)?''; \emph{Ghost Story (1981)} as ``Have you seen Battlestar Galactica (1980)?'';
\emph{Desperate Hours (1955)} as ``What did you think of Bunted, The (1941)?''. The era and register
are neighbouring and the film is wrong; these are Hewitt et al.'s machine-only
synonyms~\cite{hewitt2025neologism}, appearing unprompted and now in fully askable form.

These strings motivate our argument against grading rendered questions by human plausibility, and the
argument is symmetric. A rater shown ``Have you seen Pulp Fiction (1995)?'' without the source vector
has one verdict available: the question is fluent, natural, answerable, and about the wrong film. The
panel marks it wrong while the instrument records that it reproduces the vector's effect. The converse
direction is the more consequential one: a renderer that ignored its input entirely and emitted ``Have
you seen The Godfather (1972)?'' at every state would satisfy raters and score at chance under the
instrument. A rater without access to the source vector therefore cannot distinguish a faithful
renderer from a fluent confabulator in either direction, so plausibility judgements are not
informative about faithfulness, and adding raters does not repair this. The panel is nonetheless the
field's standard instrument, with a hundred raters for ELM~\cite{tennenholtz2023elm}, a hundred and
twenty-four for EAGLE~\cite{tennenholtz2024eagle}, and an expert discrimination test for
ctELM~\cite{ondov2026ctelm}. What a human study could decide is Baum and Lang's actual question,
namely whether a person can answer the query at all.

\section{Closing the loop, and what it costs}
\label{sec:loop}

At each of the $42$ states with a full exact gain table we take the blend target, render it, and push
the rendered word back through the independent bridge to determine whether it carries the direction
the point was built from. The reference $R_s$ is the state's own measured ingredient direction, so no
quantity here is an answer to a question.

\paragraph{The rendered words carry their own point.} A render of a given point aligns with that
point's reference better than a render of another point does, at $+0.28593$
$[+0.17820,\,+0.39777]$, with the prior floor at zero ($-0.013$ $[-0.074,\,+0.053]$). A second,
independent view asks whether the rendered word is among the point's own nearest concepts: where the
decoder produces something distinctive ($18$ of $42$ states) it lands at median rank $91$ of
$1{,}031$ in the point's own neighbourhood, with several exact hits, namely \texttt{action} rank $1$,
\texttt{art} $4$, \texttt{indictment} $5$ (a courtroom blend of \emph{Primal Fear} and \emph{A Time to
Kill}), \texttt{animals} $7$ (the Pixar branch), \texttt{military} $21$, \texttt{adventure} $28$ (a
Harry Potter blend), \texttt{science} $31$, \texttt{noir} $54$. Where greedy falls back on one of
three stock words the median rank is $362.5$, no better than the random-tag floor of $340$, a
generation-diversity limit that \S\ref{sec:cost} removes by changing the decoder alone.

\paragraph{The catalogue does not express the direction.} At each state we take the bank entry nearest
the emitted point in the emitter's own head space, at cosine $0.93$. Its own gold phrase, through the
same bridge, is anti-aligned with the point's measured direction at $-0.158$ $[-0.224,\,-0.089]$,
while the rendered off-catalogue word carries it at $+0.204$ $[+0.117,\,+0.291]$: a gap of $+0.362$
with the interval excluding zero. Two caveats travel with the magnitude wherever it is quoted. The
$k(\varphi)$-nearest entry is itself a common-mode artefact of an anisotropic space, and is frequently
the same entry, \texttt{divorce}; and the bridge favours concept-flavoured text, which places a thumb
on the scale for a one-word render against a bank phrase. The sign survives both; the magnitude is not
to be quoted without them.

\subsection{What the translation costs}
\label{sec:cost}

Two arms run under the same declared-circular simulator and the same certified fold operator,
differing only in what is folded: arm~$L$ folds the direction's own top concepts, and arm~$T$ folds
the concepts recovered from the rendered text. The circular component is common to both and the
difference is the transport cost. Translating the direction into English and reading it back costs
$+0.01802$ full $[+0.01668,\,+0.01940]$ and $+0.00672$ tail $[+0.00541,\,+0.00805]$ NDCG@10 over
$36{,}197$ user-rows across $42$ nested states. It is a genuine semantic-transport measure rather than
the two arms folding the same content: $|L\cap T|$ is zero at $38$ of $42$ states. It is non-uniform,
with $21$ states showing an interval above zero and $5$ showing one below it, the latter being states
at which the English beats the direction, the strongest case being \texttt{theatrical} at $-0.079$
$[-0.119,\,-0.040]$. It is also largely removable by decoding alone (Table~\ref{tab:loop}).

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Emission is licensed only where the question landscape was measured ($72$ of $144$ nodes): at withheld
states the trained emission is beaten by a state-blind constant, and the state-specific component,
substantial at $59\%$ $[55.6\%,\,62.7\%]$ of mean-removed target energy, was not recoverable from the
state by any method we tried. Every adapter is a single training seed, so we do not use the word
robust. $5.3\%$ of held-out generations are malformed, and the transcript shows templating degrading
further on blends. The translation cost is measured under a declared-circular answer model with both
arms sharing it; only the difference is read, and no absolute row is headlined. The bridge that scores
faithfulness is concept-routed, which makes the item channel an instrument limit rather than a finding
about items. There is no human study: a plausibility panel would penalise exactly the machine-only
synonyms that carry the vector and reward a fluent confabulator that carries none, so the human study
worth running is Baum and Lang's answerability question, not quality grading.

% =====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A cold-start interview can ask only what its catalogue contains, and we measured the cost of that
constraint: at the states we study the nearest catalogue question's own text points away from the
direction the measured gains indicate, while a word produced at that state carries it. The missing
component was a renderer, and language is one. A small adapter into a frozen language model expresses
a synthesised collaborative direction in English, the vector demonstrably conditions what it says, the
result survives being made askable at $94.7\%$, and a six-turn interview runs end to end, asking about
sci-fi where the catalogue would ask about alien films. What makes this an audit rather than a
demonstration is the apparatus: a correspondence-scrambled twin, a same-instrument ceiling that prices
naturalness at $23\%$ of verifiability, a trivial floor beside every headline, and a plug-in causal
measure of whether the text does what the vector did. That last is what we would most like the field
to take up, because a panel judging a rendered latent by how it reads cannot tell our renderer from a
confabulator.
