\title{\textbf{A Question the Catalogue Does Not Contain:\\
Emitting, Rendering and Auditing Off-Catalogue Elicitation Questions}}
\author{}
\date{September 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent A cold-start recommender that interviews its user can only ask what is in its question
catalogue. We show that the catalogue is the binding constraint, and that it can be removed. At the
interview states we study, the nearest catalogue question is close to the policy's emitted direction
in the policy's own head space (cosine $0.93$) --- and yet that question's \emph{own text} is
anti-aligned with the direction the measured gains point to, at $-0.158$ $[-0.224,\,-0.089]$, while
an off-catalogue word rendered on the spot carries it at $+0.204$ $[+0.117,\,+0.291]$. We then build
the missing renderer. A two-layer adapter into a frozen language model says such a direction in
English, and the vector genuinely conditions the text: against a correspondence-scrambled twin,
$+0.111$ $[+0.105,\,+0.117]$ through an independently fitted bridge, with the true arm at $107\%$ of
the gold phrase's own ceiling and source identification $0.678$ (chance $0.5$). Retrained on
question-shaped targets it emits well-formed questions $94.7\%$ of the time and the conditioning
survives; templating costs $23\%$ of the instrument's ceiling, a price the verbalisation literature
has not reported because it does not measure against a same-instrument ceiling. Closing the loop, a
synthesised direction is rendered, folded back, and costs $+0.018$ NDCG@10 in transport under a
declared-circular answer model shared by both arms. The claim is capability: a collaborative latent,
rendered as language, left unsnapped.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A cold-start recommender that interviews its user has to ask something, and everything it can ask is
on a list. In this system the list has $19{,}390$ entries --- $18{,}359$ films and $1{,}031$
concepts --- and the interview problem reduces to an argmax over it: which entry splits this cohort,
which one a person can answer, which order pays. The list is the action space, and an action space is
a comfortable thing to have.

This paper is about the moment that comfort breaks. Three questions in --- no answer to \emph{do you
like better-than-expected films?}, no answer on \emph{True Lies}, a dislike for \emph{Apollo 13} ---
the policy's own machinery computes a direction it would like to probe, and then does the only thing
it can: it snaps that direction to the closest thing on the list and asks that instead. How much is
lost in the snap turns out to be measurable. At the interview states we study, the nearest catalogue entry is close to the
emitted direction in the policy's own head space --- cosine $0.93$, as close as the catalogue gets.
Pushed through a bridge fitted independently of everything else here, \textbf{that entry's own gold
phrase is anti-aligned with the direction the measured gains point to, at $-0.158$
$[-0.224,\,-0.089]$, while a word invented on the spot by a small language model reading that same
direction carries it at $+0.204$ $[+0.117,\,+0.291]$} --- a gap of $+0.362$ with the interval
excluding zero (\S\ref{sec:loop}; two caveats are attached there). The direction is expressible in
English. The catalogue does not contain the sentence that expresses it.

The rest of the paper earns the right to show that contrast and audits what the renderer produces.
\S\ref{sec:point} takes the emitted point: a policy trained on gain-weighted blends of the candidates
whose value was \emph{measured} at each state emits off-catalogue directions while still running the
tree's ordinary interview. \S\ref{sec:english} trains an adapter into a frozen \texttt{distilgpt2}
and tests the only thing that matters about it --- whether the text depends on the vector --- against
a twin trained identically on \emph{scrambled} vector--text pairs, first on bare phrases and then on
question-shaped targets. \S\ref{sec:loop} closes the loop and prices it; \S\ref{sec:demo} runs a full
synthesised interview.

\paragraph{What is claimed, as a conjunction.} Every leg has prior art and we claim no leg alone.
Every prior method that uses belief geometry to choose an elicitation question reduces the covariance
to a \emph{scalar} and uses it to \emph{score a finite pool}; we take the argmax \emph{direction
itself} as the emitted object, render it as language, and audit what comes out. The unoccupied
conjunction is a \textbf{collaborative} latent, rendered as \textbf{language}, left
\textbf{unsnapped} --- the decoded point chosen by an interview policy at an interview state, the
renderer audited against a correspondence-scrambled latent, and faithfulness scored causally by
substituting the text where the latent was. The claim is \textbf{capability, not value}: a direction
outside the bank can be emitted, said and audited; we never claim that asking it improves a
recommendation.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Choosing a question from belief geometry --- not ours.} The principal-axis rule (probe along
the leading eigenvector of the posterior covariance) is old and overdetermined: Ji, Xue and Carin
state it verbatim, \emph{and} state the synthesise-or-snap fork itself --- if such flexibility is not
feasible, design a library of possible next projections~\cite{ji2008bcs}. Info-Synth reaches the same
direction from mutual information under an ideal-point probit~\cite{nadagouda2026infosynth}; Zhang et
al.\ adaptively minimise the trace of the posterior covariance of a respondent's own latent
factor~\cite{zhang2020activemf}; Eigentaste elicits on principal components of the rating
matrix~\cite{goldberg2001eigentaste}; Fonarev et al.\ pick elicitation items by maximal
volume~\cite{fonarev2016rectangular}; the blend target we use as supervision is DGRL's
construction~\cite{hoppe2026dgrl}. What is left is what happens \emph{after} the argmax direction is
computed.\footnote{Loepp, Hussein and Ziegler (CHI 2014) reportedly elicits preferences by presenting
item sets at the extremes of each latent factor in turn. We could not obtain the paper through three
routes and therefore make no novelty claim about interrogating along a latent direction.}

\paragraph{Answerability: a thirty-four-year-old failure, and its two fixes.} Baum and Lang showed in
1992 that a learner which synthesises the maximally informative input and asks a human to label it
can work poorly for a reason better optimisation does not fix: the informative point is a fusion of
two classes, a person cannot label it, and the query returns noise~\cite{baum1992query}. Zhu and
Bento reproduced it with GANs, reporting that labellers failed to confidently identify most generated
images~\cite{zhu2017gaal}. The two standard responses are exactly our two options: \textbf{snap to a
real neighbour}, or \textbf{verbalise}. Snapping is the majority answer, and inside recommendation
Vendrov et al.\ do precisely it --- optimise continuous hypothetical item vectors, then project back
to the nearest real items~\cite{vendrov2020gradient}; removing the snap is the risk we take, not a
shortcut we decline. Where a design can be \emph{physically executed} the synthesised query does reach
people, because there the world renders it: LineCoSpar proposes a gait from no pool and has an
exoskeleton walk it for six real subjects~\cite{tucker2020linecospar}, and adaptive design
optimisation does the same in psychophysics~\cite{ahn2020ado}. Recommendation has no such renderer,
which is why the field snapped to a catalogue --- and why we propose that \emph{language is the
missing one}.

\paragraph{Verbalising latents, and the validation gap.} ELM maps vectors from a frozen
recommendation model into a language model's token-embedding space and describes them, including
synthesised interpolations~\cite{tennenholtz2023elm}; we take that mechanism rather than invent one.
EAGLE steers a frozen LLM toward high-utility regions of a MovieLens-25M behavioural embedding and
has \emph{no decoder} --- it edits text and encodes the result, so no latent point is ever mapped to
language, and its authors name the price: coverage is limited by the action
space~\cite{tennenholtz2024eagle}. GeneRec claims off-corpus generation in the \emph{item}
channel~\cite{wang2023generec}, ours is the question channel; GenMQD generates a textual description
of a wanted instance in vision active learning~\cite{genmqd2024}. DeCap gives the measured argument
for a soft snap over a hard one --- CIDEr $8.1$ for a raw off-manifold vector, $27.1$ for a hard
nearest-neighbour snap, $42.1$ for a softmax-weighted blend~\cite{li2023decap} --- and round-trip
cosine alone is not evidence, since zero-shot inverters reach cosine above $90$ at F1 only above
$50$~\cite{zhang2025zsinvert}. The validation gap is one control, stated narrowly: \textbf{we are
aware of no embedding-verbalisation system that reports what its renderer produces from a
correspondence-scrambled latent.} The construction is not ours to claim --- Heap et al.\ found
automated interpretability scores \emph{higher} for randomly initialised transformers ($0.87$ against
a trained $0.79$) while the only null that separated, at chance $0.50$, scrambled the token-embedding
correspondence~\cite{heap2026autointerp}, and Tang et al.\ permute voxel responses to destroy
response-to-stimulus correspondence while preserving response statistics~\cite{tang2023semantic}. Its
\emph{application} to a rendered elicitation question, where no paired reference text exists, is
ours. The metric we import is Hewitt et al.'s causal one: substitute the text back where the latent
was and measure how much of the latent's effect it reproduces~\cite{hewitt2025neologism}.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

Nothing in the instrument is refitted. The recommender checkpoint, the fold-in operator and the
question bank are frozen certified artifacts; the bank holds $19{,}390$ questions in two channels
($18{,}359$ items, $1{,}031$ concepts); the ruler is full and tail NDCG@10 on the canonical split's
$10{,}000$ validation users, and the $10{,}000$ canonical test users were not touched. The base policy
reads the interview state, emits a continuous query vector $\zhat$, and asks whichever bank question
scores highest against it under $s(q\mid\text{state}) = \langle u(h),k(\varphi_q)\rangle +
b(\varphi_q)$; we measure in homogeneous coordinates so that $\langle\zhat,e_q\rangle$ reproduces
$s$ exactly. Faithfulness is measured through a \textbf{tower bridge}, a text-to-collaborative map
fitted independently of both the emitter and the renderer, so no reading is closed over the system's
own components. Three conventions bind every number below: every statistic carries an interval and
declares its aggregation unit; every headline number is printed beside its trivial floor (state-blind
constant, random direction, prior-only, or chance); and every registered reading rule was applied to
its own null at design time.

\paragraph{The circularity contract.} The simulated answerer puts the policy at interview states and
does nothing else, with one declared exception: the translation-cost measurement of \S\ref{sec:cost}
needs an answer model by construction. There we follow the field's practice --- Info-Synth's
geometry-dependent response model~\cite{nadagouda2026infosynth}, PEBOL's simulated
respondents~\cite{austin2024bayesian} --- declare the circularity in our own voice, put both arms
under the \emph{same} simulator, and read only the difference. Absolute rows are printed and
labelled; no version of ``asking the synthesised question improves recommendations'' appears here.

\section{The emitted point}
\label{sec:point}

The deployed policy's emitted vector never lies on the question it selects: over all $160{,}000$
validation states the \emph{maximum} cosine to the snapped entry is $0.1203$. That falsification is
unconditional, but the raw geometry is dominated by a common mode --- cross-entropy constrains only
score \emph{differences}, so the head is free to place a large shared offset in $k(\varphi)$, and it
did (median top score $-82.7$; the bank's mean pairwise cosine is $0.964$ raw against $0.0018$
centred). Mean-removed, the picture becomes informative: the emission's cosine to the entry it selects
has median $+0.488$ against $+0.170$ for a norm-matched random direction. \textbf{The trained emission
is a large common-mode offset plus a genuine bank-aligned direction} --- but it is a scoring
functional, not a target in question space. To give it one, we retrain: we replace the one-hot
distillation target with a \textbf{gain-weighted blend of the top-$m$ candidates whose value was
measured at that state}, so the target is off-catalogue by construction while every gradient still
comes from a question we actually measured, trained jointly with a replay cross-entropy toward the
tree's ordinary picks so the object remains a policy. It works, and as one object: the retrained
policy runs the tree's ordinary interview essentially unchanged (Table~\ref{tab:point}) \emph{and}
emits blends whose median top-1 ingredient weight is $0.2254$, with $0$ of $72$ states above $0.5$,
against a random floor of $0.121$.

\section{Saying it in English}
\label{sec:english}

The renderer is a two-layer MLP producing eight soft prefix tokens into a \textbf{frozen}
\texttt{distilgpt2}, trained on the $19{,}390$ bank entries with a $20\%$ identity-held-out split
stratified by channel and popularity decile. The load-bearing control is a \textbf{twin trained
identically on correspondence-scrambled pairs}: same architecture, data and optimiser, with a
channel-preserving permutation of which vector goes with which text. This is the only renderer
variant under which ``the vector conditions what is said'' is literally true, and therefore the only
one in which scrambling tests \emph{rendering} rather than \emph{retrieval}. The arms separate by
epoch~2 in both runs.

\paragraph{The mechanism, on bare phrases.} On $3{,}670$ held-out item questions, through the
independent bridge, the true adapter's text re-encodes toward its own held-out vector at
$\mathbf{+0.1114}$ $[+0.1054,\,+0.1173]$ above the scrambled twin, with the true arm at
$\mathbf{107\%}$ of what the \emph{gold phrase itself} earns through the same instrument, and it
identifies its source vector against a same-channel decoy at $\mathbf{0.6778}$
$[0.6631,\,0.6931]$ --- above the gold phrase's own $0.6624$. The answer to the question the design
asked is yes: the text generated from a vector depends on that vector, on held-out questions, through
an independently fitted bridge, per channel, with intervals.

\paragraph{It survives being made askable.} An interview asks \emph{questions}, so we retrain the
same adapter on question-shaped targets built from the same bank entries with a small per-channel
template family, one template sampled per example per epoch. Everything else is held fixed, including
the scrambled twin. \textbf{$94.7\%$ $[0.9398,\,0.9542]$ of held-out generations are well-formed
questions} under a rule fixed in advance, and every registered reading passes under \emph{both}
decodings and on both channels (Table~\ref{tab:english}).

\paragraph{Naturalness costs verifiability, and we can price it.} The gate is calibrated against what
the true text earns through the same instrument, so a change of output format is a change of
instrument and the ceiling must be re-measured. It moves a long way. \textbf{Wrapping a phrase in
question boilerplate lowers what a \emph{perfect} rendering can score by $23\%$ on items
($0.08920\to0.06828$) and $17\%$ on concepts}: the words that make a question askable are the same
words for every question in the channel, so they add surface carrying no information about the
direction. This is why the bare-phrase adapter's $107\%$ of ceiling and the question adapter's $71\%$
are not two readings of one number. Reporting a figure of this kind requires a same-instrument
ceiling --- a measurement of what the ground-truth text itself earns through the identical chain ---
which is exactly the calibration the verbalisation literature does not run. Hence the rule:
\textbf{re-measure the ceiling before reading any gate under a changed output format}.

\paragraph{Machine-only synonyms, and why a plausibility panel cannot adjudicate this.} Content
preservation is effectively zero --- exact content match $0.0003$, token-F1 $0.055$ --- and the vector
is carried anyway, at $61\%$ identification against chance $50\%$ and a ceiling of $0.6316$. The
failures are legible: \emph{The Shawshank Redemption} renders as \textbf{``Have you seen Pulp Fiction
(1995)?''}; \emph{Ghost Story (1981)} as \textbf{``Have you seen Battlestar Galactica (1980)?''};
\emph{Desperate Hours (1955)} as \textbf{``What did you think of Bunted, The (1941)?''}. Neighbouring
era, neighbouring register, wrong film --- Hewitt et al.'s machine-only
synonyms~\cite{hewitt2025neologism} appearing unprompted, now in fully askable form.

These strings are our argument against grading rendered questions by human plausibility, and the
argument is \textbf{symmetric}. A rater shown ``Have you seen Pulp Fiction (1995)?'' without the
source vector has one verdict available: fluent, natural, perfectly answerable, and about the wrong
film. The panel marks it wrong; the instrument records that it reproduces the vector's effect. The
other direction is the one that should worry the field: \textbf{a renderer that ignored its input
entirely and emitted ``Have you seen The Godfather (1972)?'' at every state would delight raters and
score at chance with the instrument.} A plausibility panel --- the field's standard instrument, a
hundred raters for ELM~\cite{tennenholtz2023elm}, a hundred and twenty-four for
EAGLE~\cite{tennenholtz2024eagle}, an expert discrimination test for ctELM~\cite{ondov2026ctelm} ---
therefore cannot separate our renderer from a confabulator in \emph{either} direction, and adding
raters does not fix it. What a human study \emph{could} decide is Baum and Lang's actual question:
can a person answer this at all.

\section{Closing the loop, and what it costs}
\label{sec:loop}

At each of the $42$ states with a full exact gain table we take the blend target, render it, and push
the rendered word back through the independent bridge to ask whether it carries the direction the
point was built from. The reference $R_s$ is the state's own measured ingredient direction, so
nothing here is an answer to a question.

\paragraph{The words are right.} A render of \emph{this} point aligns with \emph{this} point's
reference better than a render of some other point does, at $\mathbf{+0.28593}$
$[+0.17820,\,+0.39777]$, with the prior floor at zero ($-0.013$ $[-0.074,\,+0.053]$). A second,
independent view asks whether the rendered word is one of the point's own nearest concepts: where the
decoder says something distinctive ($18$ of $42$ states) it lands at \textbf{median rank $91$ of
$1{,}031$} in the point's own neighbourhood, with genuine bullseyes --- \texttt{action} rank
$\mathbf{1}$, \texttt{art} $4$, \texttt{indictment} $5$ (a courtroom blend of \emph{Primal Fear} and
\emph{A Time to Kill}), \texttt{animals} $7$ (the Pixar branch), \texttt{military} $21$,
\texttt{adventure} $28$ (a Harry Potter blend), \texttt{science} $31$, \texttt{noir} $54$. Where
greedy falls back on one of three stock words the median rank is $362.5$, no better than the
random-tag floor of $340$ --- a generation-diversity limit that \S\ref{sec:cost} removes by changing
the decoder alone.

\paragraph{The catalogue cannot say it.} At each state we take the bank entry nearest the emitted
point in the emitter's own head space --- cosine $0.93$. Its own gold phrase, through the same bridge,
is \textbf{anti-aligned with the point's measured direction at $-0.158$ $[-0.224,\,-0.089]$}, while
the rendered off-catalogue word carries it at $\mathbf{+0.204}$ $[+0.117,\,+0.291]$: a gap of
$+0.362$, interval excluding zero. Two caveats travel with the magnitude wherever it is quoted. The
$k(\varphi)$-nearest entry is itself a common-mode artefact of an anisotropic space --- it is
frequently the \emph{same} entry, \texttt{divorce} --- and the bridge favours concept-flavoured text,
a thumb on the scale for a one-word render against a bank phrase. The sign survives both; the
magnitude is not to be quoted without them.

\subsection{What the translation costs}
\label{sec:cost}

Two arms under the \emph{same} declared-circular simulator and the same certified fold operator,
differing only in what is folded: arm~$L$ folds the direction's own top concepts, arm~$T$ folds the
concepts recovered from the rendered text. The circular component is common to both and the
difference is the transport cost. \textbf{Translating the direction into English and reading it back
costs $+0.01802$ full $[+0.01668,\,+0.01940]$ and $+0.00672$ tail $[+0.00541,\,+0.00805]$ NDCG@10}
over $36{,}197$ user-rows across $42$ nested states. It is a genuine semantic-transport measure
rather than the two arms folding the same thing: $|L\cap T|$ is zero at $38$ of $42$ states. It is
non-uniform --- $21$ states have an interval above zero and \textbf{$5$ have one below it}, states
where the English beats the direction, the best being \texttt{theatrical} at $-0.079$
$[-0.119,\,-0.040]$. And it is largely removable by decoding alone (Table~\ref{tab:loop}).

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Emission is licensed only where the question landscape was measured ($72$ of $144$ nodes): at
withheld states the trained emission is beaten by a state-blind constant, and the state-specific
component --- substantial at $59\%$ $[55.6\%,\,62.7\%]$ of mean-removed target energy --- was not
recoverable from the state by any method we tried. Every adapter is a single training seed, so we do
not use the word robust. $5.3\%$ of held-out generations are malformed, and the transcript shows
templating degrading further on blends. The translation cost is measured under a declared-circular
answer model with both arms sharing it; only the difference is read, and no absolute row is
headlined. The bridge that scores faithfulness is concept-routed, which makes the item channel an
instrument limit rather than a finding about items. There is no human study: a plausibility panel
would penalise exactly the machine-only synonyms that carry the vector and reward a fluent
confabulator that carries none, so the human study worth running is Baum and Lang's answerability
question, not quality grading.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A cold-start interview can only ask what is in its catalogue, and we measured what that costs: at the
states we study the nearest catalogue question's own text points away from the direction the data
says to probe, while a word invented on the spot carries it. The missing piece was a renderer, and
language is one. A small adapter into a frozen language model says a synthesised collaborative
direction in English, the vector demonstrably conditions what it says, the result survives being made
askable at $94.7\%$, and a six-turn interview runs end to end asking about sci-fi where the catalogue
would ask about alien films. What makes this an audit rather than a demo is the apparatus: a
correspondence-scrambled twin, a same-instrument ceiling that prices naturalness at $23\%$ of
verifiability, a trivial floor beside every headline, and a plug-in causal measure of whether the
text does what the vector did. That last is what we would most like the field to take up --- because
a panel judging a rendered latent by how it reads cannot tell our renderer from a confabulator.
