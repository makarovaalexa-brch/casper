# Meta-review (Area Chair) — "Objective Mismatch in Adaptive Preference Elicitation for Cold-Start Recommendation"

## Summary of contribution

The paper re-implements thirteen published cold-start question-selection methods (twenty-three arms) under one unusually well-specified measurement contract: a frozen certified two-tower recommender near RecVAE parity, ML-25M under the Liang strong-generalisation split, answers read behaviourally from each user's own held-out ratings, the full 18,359-item catalogue ranked without sampling, unanswerable questions charged, 10,000 disjoint test users, clustered paired bootstrap. Against a hard static comparator — the greedy prefix-optimal question order built by directly maximising the reported metric, full NDCG@10 — every published selector loses at every budget from K=1 to K=16. The paper then diagnoses the cause and fixes it. The diagnosis (RQ2) scores three published split criteria against exact measured NDCG@10 gains at the 22 states an interview tree visits: they correlate at +0.010, −0.060 and −0.104 and recover only 11–34% of what a uniform random pick forgoes. The fix (RQ3) is CASPER: Golbandi's ternary tree with exactly one thing changed — the scalar scoring a candidate split becomes measured cohort-mean held-out NDCG@10 — which reverses the RQ1 comparison (+0.01035 full, +0.00705 tail at K=16), survives a six-arm one-variable-changed ablation where every surrogate and two shuffle controls lose, and holds on three of four answer consumers. A self-undercutting final result shows that enlarging the bank with Tag-Genome concept questions, with no conditioning at all, buys more (+0.01430 full, +0.02199 tail), the mechanism being answerability (12.36 answers per sixteen concept questions against 3.11 per sixteen item questions).

## Decision

**Weak accept**, confidence high (I read the paper closely and am familiar with the elicitation literature it audits).

*To the authors:* The paper is accepted because it does not stop at the negative result — the criterion-regret table and the one-variable-changed ablation convert "published methods lose" into a mechanism and a fix, and the fix is priced honestly on the same contract, including against a bank change that beats it.

## Strongest reason to accept

This paper closes a hole the field has left open for fifteen years, with evidence nobody else has assembled. Since the reported metric moved from RMSE to truncated top-k ranking, *no* adaptive selector has been tested against a static order maximising that metric — and the one prior attempt to include the rating-era interview methods on ML-25M/NDCG@10 could not get them running at all. The authors got thirteen running, gated five natively, reported the gate failures instead of burying them (EDDI reduced and marked †, Greedy SLIM excluded), and produced the object the field has never had: 22 nodes × ~1,000 candidates of *exact measured* NDCG@10 gain — the ground truth every split criterion has only ever approximated, and a permanent reusable artefact. On top of it sits the cleanest single-variable experiment I have seen in this literature: same tree code, branch rule, leaf size, recommender, pool, mask, cohort, budget, only the scoring scalar changes — and that one number flips the sign of the conditioning gain. The paper also refuses the cheap headline available to it, stating that a fixed order *is* an adaptive policy ignoring its inputs, so no measurement can place adaptivity below staticness, only particular methods. That is the discipline this venue should reward.

## Strongest reason to reject

The entire result rests on a comparator searched on the reported metric, on the very recommender that then scores it — and no ported arm is allowed anything comparable. The authors concede this in one Section 7 sentence and mitigate it with bandit constant tuning worth +0.0058 against a 0.037–0.044 deficit: a real mitigation for the bandits, none at all for the tree-based and design-based methods. A hostile reading of RQ1 is therefore *a method that optimises X beats methods that optimise Y when you score on X*, close to tautological — and the paper's own related work admits Sepliarskaia et al. (2018) already showed static questionnaires beating a dynamic one optimising a non-loss objective. Worse, no published number validates the comparator, and rebuilding it under the scoring contract already moved the headline conditioning gain from +0.01371 to +0.01035: roughly a quarter of the effect was comparator weakness, direct evidence that the comparator is a moving, unaudited object. Add gains of 0.010–0.014 on a base of ~0.259, six of fifteen leaves losing on test over 39.3% of users, validation overstating the effect nearly threefold, and a null pre-registered routing test — and the positive contribution is a small, fragile margin around a large, impeccably executed negative one.

## Genuine advance or well-executed audit?

Both, and the proportions matter. Sections 4 and 5 are an audit — an outstanding one, but an audit of others' methods, and by itself a reproducibility contribution rather than an ECIR full-paper one. Section 6 makes it a full paper: CASPER is constructive and mechanistically motivated, validated by a six-arm ablation with two shuffle controls, and it transfers across consumers. Honestly accounted, the paper is roughly 60% audit and 40% advance, and the 40% is load-bearing. That matters here because the new knowledge lives in the 40%: that the separating axis is *measured versus surrogate*, not ranking versus rating (the pairwise-versus-variance null is the sharpest finding in the paper and was, the authors say, unregistered), and that the failure is in the Taylor approximation rather than the shape of the loss (+0.784 for the measured pairwise loss against −0.149 for its trained approximation). That pair is a genuine advance, independent of who wins the table.

## Position against the ECIR bar

Upper half of the second quartile: above the accept line on rigour and on contribution, but not top-decile. What holds it back is that the positive delta is small, single-dataset, single-instrument, and won by a comparator whose fairness the authors cannot fully defend. What would move it up one quartile is a second dataset or question bank showing the criterion-regret finding is not an ML-25M/Tag-Genome artefact, or — cheaper and nearly as convincing — giving one strong published selector the same held-out metric-search budget the comparator gets and showing it still loses. Either converts "our objective wins when we score on our objective" into "the objective mismatch is the mechanism."

## Three changes required before camera-ready

1. **Promote the comparator-fairness asymmetry from a Section 7 sentence to a subsection with a number.** State before Table 1 exactly what search budget the static order received and what each ported arm received, and run at least one arm with a matched metric-driven search over its own free parameters. The +0.01371 → +0.01035 rebuild is the right kind of evidence and is currently buried in Limitations; move it forward and expand it. *Required.*
2. **Reframe the abstract so the audit is the setup and CASPER plus the answerability result is the claim.** As written it leads with "every published method loses," the tautology-adjacent half; the durable half is the criterion-regret table, the measured-versus-surrogate axis, and the bank change beating conditioning. Also state in the abstract, not only Section 6, that the tree shows no routing (pre-registered congruence null). *Required.*
3. **Report cost and reach alongside every headline gain.** The 37,191 s/tree against 11 s for the Taylor construction, the 39.3% of users in losing leaves, and the ~14% answering nothing in three questions are scattered; one table pairing gain with cost and coverage lets a practitioner judge whether +0.01035 is worth 3,400× the compute. *Strongly advised.*

## Reproducibility track?

No. The paper would pass that track easily, but it is not the right home. That track rewards faithful re-execution; this re-execution is explicitly *not* faithful to the originals' protocols — it deliberately transplants thirteen methods onto a contract none was designed for, and the authors say the ablation "is not a refutation of the published results on their own protocols." A frame built on protocol fidelity is wrong for a study whose point is that moving to the metric the field now reports breaks the methods. Add that CASPER is a new selector with a positive result, and the full-paper track is correct. I would, however, require the anonymised artefact mirror they promise; the footnote asking reviewers to disregard the missing repository is not something I would accept twice.

## Over- and under-claiming

*Over-claiming.* "Frontier recommender" does more work than 0.3496/0.2491 against an in-house RecVAE bar of 0.3540/0.2497 supports — the instrument is *below* its own bar on both axes and is certified only against a technical report reviewers cannot see (ref. [3], "under review"). Soften to "parity with a strong in-house baseline." "First" in contribution one needs qualifying given Sepliarskaia et al. The RecVAE column in Table 5 (+0.04122) must not be quoted as a headline: a static arm reading .0000/.0000 at K=2 means that consumer is degenerate at low budgets, and a gain over a degenerate arm is not comparable to the certified tower's +0.01035.

*Under-claiming.* Three findings are stronger than their presentation. That upgrading a rating surrogate to a ranking surrogate buys literally nothing (+0.00020, the only null in the matrix) is more useful to the field than the CASPER margin and is currently a "First," inside a paragraph. The Taylor-approximation diagnosis (+0.784 measured versus −0.149 approximated, same objective) identifies precisely where fifteen years of methods went wrong and deserves its own figure. And the depth-is-not-conditioning control (the shuffle grows deeper, wider and more varied and still loses by −0.04817) refutes a diagnostic the field routinely relies on, yet is one clause in a long paragraph. Foregrounding these three would also materially strengthen the answer to the "this is just an audit" objection.
