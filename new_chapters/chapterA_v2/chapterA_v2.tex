\begin{abstract}
\noindent \textbf{The published ranking of cold-start question-selection heuristics is not a property of the
heuristics.} Holding the questions, the users, the answers and the candidate pool fixed and varying only the
recommender that consumes the answers, Rashid et al.'s reported ordering reproduces under the node model of
Golbandi et al.\ and \emph{inverts} under a frontier-accuracy consumer---on both metrics, with every gap that
decides an ordering separated by a paired per-user bootstrap. An elicitation result is therefore a claim about a
strategy \emph{and} the recommender it was measured through, and the field has largely reported the pair as though
it were the strategy.

Establishing that requires the thing it is a claim about. Before one can study \emph{how} to interview a cold-start
user---which questions to ask, in what
order---one needs a recommender that can \emph{be} interviewed: a model that folds an arbitrary-length,
mixed-channel set of answers into its user representation and re-ranks a large catalogue, without per-user retraining and
without trading away full-profile accuracy. We argue that this recommender is a \emph{measuring instrument} for the
elicitation-policy literature, and that its requirements are more demanding than any single published system we
surveyed meets. The demanding part is \emph{ingestion}: folding an arbitrary-length set of mixed-type answers into a recommender
that is already at the accuracy frontier. We state that as four requirements---\textbf{R1} frontier-parity
full-profile accuracy, \textbf{R2} an open set-valued input interface (any number of observations, and directions
that need not have existed when the model was fitted---concepts, entities, phrases, policy-emitted vectors),
\textbf{R3} monotonicity (a truthful answer does not lower ranking
quality beyond noise), and \textbf{R4} answers that carry sign and intensity---and an
acceptance battery (one verification item per requirement, \textbf{R1--R4}, plus two integrity items) that
operationalises them.

R2 carries most of the weight: it is what the instrument \emph{provides} to a policy, and what
a policy should do with it is the companion chapter's subject. A survey of six model families shows the literature is \emph{bifurcated}: the models that hit
the full-profile bar (shallow linear/autoencoder recommenders such as EASE~\cite{steck2019ease} and
RecVAE~\cite{ren2020recvae}) are item-only point estimators, while the models with an uncertainty-native, multi-channel
belief (soft-attribute Gaussian elicitation~\cite{biyik2023soft}, unified item$+$attribute bandits~\cite{li2021seamlessly},
conjugate critique folds~\cite{toroghi2023bcie}) do not report full-profile accuracy at the R1 bar. We are aware of no
published system that occupies the conjunction of all four.

The instrument that closes this gap is a \emph{frozen} certified
shallow frontier tower over whose latent every channel is folded as a direction paired with a value by a
permutation-invariant set encoder; three of its input legs---signed/graded values, concepts
whose vocabulary is not fixed when the model is fitted, and continuous direction tokens---have no faithful encoding in the item-indicator basis of the R1-bar
models. Each requirement carries one verification item, R1--R4, and all four are met.

This chapter contributes the requirement framework, the taxonomy and gap analysis, the instrument's design,
and the experimental protocol (a metric-certification bridge to the published ML-20M numbers, then R1--R4 on the
ML-25M harness). Our in-house RecVAE realisation sets the bar at full/tail NDCG@10 $0.3540/0.2497$ on the canonical
ML-25M protocol, and the graded-native interview tower comes within $1.2\%$ of it: a single certified training run reaches
$0.3496/0.2491$ on the held-out test cohort---statistically indistinguishable on the tail, a $1.2\%$ relative shortfall on the full
profile---while reproducing the frozen
decoder's ranking exactly at the empty interview, so R1 (full strength preserved) is passed rather than assumed. The
remaining verification items have all been run and are met. With the tower fixed, a quantity the elicitation
literature has long treated qualitatively---how often a cold user can answer the question at all---becomes
directly measurable, and it does not depend on how a question bank is ranked: a held-out user has rated
$25.2\%$ of the hundred \emph{most popular} films in the catalogue and can answer $53.0\%$ of the $1{,}031$
concepts. Which channel wins turns out to depend on how the question bank is
ranked: rank it by how much a question \emph{discriminates} and the concept channel beats the item channel
on both metrics at every budget, because a maximally divisive item is one almost nobody has seen.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction: the recommender as a measuring instrument}\label{sec:intro}
% =====================================================================

A \emph{cold-start} conversational recommender must elicit taste from a short interview and then rank a large
catalogue. Two questions are entangled: \emph{what to ask} (the elicitation policy) and \emph{how to turn answers
into recommendations} (the recommender). The elicitation-policy literature is large and active, but almost every
comparison in it is confounded: a policy's apparent value depends on the recommender it drives, and reported gains
routinely conflate a better \emph{question-selection strategy} with a better \emph{answer-ingestion model}. Before we
can measure elicitation strategies in isolation, we need to fix the recommender---and not just any recommender, but one
strong and flexible enough that a good interview is not wasted on it. We call such a recommender an
\emph{interview-compatible instrument}.

\paragraph{The confound is not hypothetical, and it changes published conclusions.}
The ranking of the classical cold-start selection heuristics is not a property of the heuristics. Under the node
model of Golbandi et al.~\cite{golbandi2011adaptive}, the order Rashid et al.~\cite{rashid2002getting} report
reproduces; under a frontier-accuracy consumer it \emph{inverts}, on both full and tail NDCG@10, with every gap
that decides an ordering separated by a paired per-user bootstrap---the questions, users, answers, budget and
candidate pool held fixed throughout, so the only thing that varies is the model reading the answers
(\S\ref{sec:instrdep}). A paper reporting either ordering in isolation, with its own recommender, would be
reporting something true and non-transferable.

This is the concrete sense in which elicitation results are instrument-relative, and it is why the rest of this
chapter is about the instrument rather than about a policy: the requirements below are not desiderata, they are
conditions a recommender must satisfy before a strategy comparison measured through it means anything. We state them
first, build and certify the instrument against them (\S\ref{sec:method}--\S\ref{sec:expdesign}), and only then
return to the comparison above, once there is something to trust it with (\S\ref{sec:instrdep}).

\paragraph{What this chapter measures, and what it does not.}
The answers folded here come from recorded behaviour: an item answer is a user's own rating, a concept answer a
log watch-lift over raw counts. That is a deliberate scope, not an omission. It lets us measure exactly what a
recommender can extract from \emph{truthful, behaviour-grounded} answers, with no recommender-derived quantity
anywhere in the answer path---a property we enforce in code and state as a first-class part of the protocol
(\S\ref{sec:firewall}). What it does not measure is how a person phrases, hedges or misreads a rendered question.
Human answer noise is a property of the elicitation layer, and it belongs to the companion chapter that studies
that layer; conflating the two is how a simulator comes to flatter the system it is simulating.

\paragraph{Accuracy at full profile does not imply accuracy after an interview.} One number makes the point
concretely, and it concerns a baseline rather than our instrument, so it can be stated before the instrument is
described. The strongest full-profile model on our ruler, RecVAE~\cite{ren2020recvae}, when restricted to the
likes among the asked items---the only encoding an item-indicator input admits---scores below our instrument by
$0.029$ full NDCG@10 after eight questions, each system on its own best question-selection strategy, on
$10{,}000$ held-out users with a paired confidence interval excluding zero (\S\ref{sec:instrdep}). The same model
is \emph{above} ours at full profile. Whatever else separates the two systems, a strong full-profile score is not
by itself sufficient for a strong short interview.

\paragraph{Four requirements.}
An instrument fit for measuring conversational elicitation must simultaneously satisfy:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.4em,itemsep=1.5pt]
  \item[R1 --- frontier-parity full profile.] On a full interaction history it must rank at the level of the strongest shallow
        collaborative-filtering baselines (EASE~\cite{steck2019ease}, RecVAE~\cite{ren2020recvae}), so that
        elicitation is measured against a recommender nobody can dismiss as weak~\cite{dacrema2019progress}.
        \emph{Frontier-parity names a tolerance, not an equality}: we fix it in advance at within $1.5\%$
        relative of the strongest measured baseline on \emph{both} axes, on one shared harness. A system may
        satisfy R1 while being separably behind the bar, and ours is---the requirement is that the shortfall be
        small, stated, and bounded, not that it be statistically absent.
  \item[R2 --- an open set-valued input interface.] It must ingest an evidence set of \emph{any} cardinality, from
        $0$ (fully cold) upward, without per-user retraining---and, critically, the members of that set
        must be \emph{arbitrary embeddings rather than pre-committed ones}. Any-length input alone is undemanding: every
        recommender folds an arbitrary number of \emph{its own} columns. The requirement is that a vector the model
        never saw when it was fitted---an out-of-catalogue concept, an entity, a phrase, a direction a policy
        emits---is a legal member of the set. That demands both a set-structured input and a latent space arbitrary
        vectors can be inserted into, which is an architectural property and not a tuning one.

        \emph{Why this is an elicitation requirement and not an engineering preference.} A cold interview is rate-limited
        by what the user can actually answer, and answerability rises with abstraction: a question about a kind of
        film presupposes nothing, where a question about a particular film presupposes having seen it
        (\S\ref{sec:concept:results}). An instrument restricted to catalogue items is therefore capped by the user's
        viewing history; one restricted to a curated attribute list is capped by whoever wrote the list. Neither cap
        is a property of the user. Since abstraction has no natural ceiling---``psychological thrillers'', ``films
        about grief'', ``the quiet dignity of losing''---any vocabulary fixed at training time forecloses part of
        what a user could have told us, and which part is decided before the user arrives.
  \item[R3 --- monotonicity.] A truthful answer must not \emph{lower} ranking quality beyond noise; adding
        evidence should, in expectation, only help.
  \item[R4 --- expressive answers.] An answer must be able to say what a star rating says and an indicator cannot:
        that the user \emph{dislikes} something, and \emph{how strongly}. Sign and intensity are information a
        one-hot cannot carry at all.
\end{description}

\paragraph{The bifurcation observation.}
The central empirical fact this chapter documents is that R1 and $\{$R2, R3, R4$\}$ are \emph{anti-correlated across
the published literature}. The models that hit the full-profile bar---closed-form linear autoencoders
(EASE~\cite{steck2019ease}, EDLAE~\cite{steck2020edlae}) and amortised VAEs (Mult-VAE~\cite{liang2018variational},
RecVAE~\cite{ren2020recvae}), together with the training-free graph filters
(GF-CF~\cite{shen2021gfcf}, BSPM~\cite{choi2023bspm}, Turbo-CF~\cite{park2024turbocf}) that claim the frontier on other
corpora---are \emph{item-only point estimators} on a one-hot indicator basis. The models with a multi-channel elicitation interface---soft-attribute
Gaussian elicitation~\cite{biyik2023soft}, item$+$attribute Thompson bandits~\cite{li2021seamlessly}, conjugate critique
folds over a frozen factorisation~\cite{toroghi2023bcie}, and per-item Bayesian NL elicitation~\cite{austin2024bayesian}---do
\emph{not} report full-profile accuracy at the R1 bar. We call these the \emph{accuracy corner} and the
\emph{elicitation corner}. No published system we are aware of occupies both (\S\ref{sec:gap}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
This chapter is the instrument paper of the thesis. It does not claim a new elicitation policy (that is the companion
method chapter); it claims the \emph{recommender that makes policy measurement possible}. Concretely:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1.5pt]
  \item \textbf{One latent, many channels, at the accuracy frontier.} A frozen tower that comes within $1.2\%$ of the strongest
        shallow baseline on a shared ruler---indistinguishable on the tail, a small and stated shortfall on the full
        profile---over whose latent every channel is folded as a direction paired with a
        value: item factors, out-of-catalogue concept directions, and directions produced outside the catalogue
        entirely. The unification is of the \emph{representation}---one space, one form of observation---not of the
        code path. We frame R1$\wedge$R2$\wedge$R3$\wedge$R4 as a single requirement no published system
        meets, and give a six-family taxonomy and per-near-miss gap analysis
        (\S\ref{sec:related},~\S\ref{sec:gap}) locating which requirement each candidate fails.
  \item \textbf{Three legs the item-indicator basis cannot carry.} We isolate the three input channels that have no faithful encoding
        (not merely untuned) in the item-indicator basis of every R1-bar model: (i) signed/graded per-entity values,
        (ii) concepts whose vocabulary is not fixed at training time, and (iii) continuous direction tokens. These are the narrow, defensible novelty
        legs of the channel-agnostic interface (\S\ref{sec:gap},~\S\ref{sec:method}).
  \item \textbf{The instrument.} A design for a \emph{frozen} certified shallow frontier tower with a
        permutation-invariant set encoder over its latent, into which every channel folds as a
        direction paired with a value (\S\ref{sec:method}). Freezing is \emph{not} what separates us from the
        soft-attribute line---G\"opfert et al.~\cite{gopfert2024cav} and B\i y\i k et al.~\cite{biyik2023soft} both
        freeze the collaborative model and learn attribute directions separately, and say so explicitly. What
        separates us is an \emph{open} concept vocabulary carrying no per-concept supervision, at the R1 accuracy
        bar (\S\ref{sec:gap}).
  \item \textbf{An acceptance battery as a reusable certificate.} We define three headline outcome gates---full-strength
        preserved (R1), cold-start per-question NDCG rises on \emph{full and tail} versus random (R3), and
        a truthful answer carries sign and intensity (R4)---and, because outcome checks alone admit several false positives
        (a sign-blind fold, a circular answer model), pair them with one verification item
        per requirement, R1--R4, and two integrity items. We propose this as a portable certificate any
        interview-compatible recommender should pass (\S\ref{sec:reqs},~\S\ref{sec:expdesign}).
\end{enumerate}

We are careful throughout to distinguish what is \emph{new} (the conjunction; the frozen-certified-tower design; the
acceptance battery on a frontier tower) from what is \emph{pre-empted} and must be cited (attributes-as-directions plus a
Bayesian fold~\cite{biyik2023soft}; unified item$+$attribute belief~\cite{li2021seamlessly}; conjugate folds over a
frozen factorisation~\cite{toroghi2023bcie}; permutation-invariant set-encoders with information-gain
acquisition~\cite{ma2019eddi}; cold-start recommenders with value-carrying set input~\cite{lin2021tanp}; the
non-degradation-of-information principle~\cite{good1967principle,blackwell1953equivalent}). ``Uncertainty-native recommender'' alone is a
crowded 2024--25 area~\cite{wang2025bdecf} and is \emph{not} claimed as novel.

\section{Requirements and gates}\label{sec:reqs}
% =====================================================================

We now state the requirements and gates formally. Let the catalogue have $n$ items and let the frozen tower expose a
$d$-dimensional latent in which each item $i$ has a factor row $\Q_i\in\R^{d}$. An \emph{evidence set} after $t$
questions is a set of observations $\mathcal E_t=\{(\bphi_j, y_j)\}_{j=1}^{t}$, where $\bphi_j\in\R^{d}$ is the latent
direction of the queried entity (an item factor, a concept centroid, an entity embedding, or a continuous direction
token) and $y_j\in\R$ is the (possibly graded, possibly signed) answer. The instrument maps the evidence set to a
point $\bmu_t = f(\mathcal E_t)$ in the user latent $\uu\in\R^{d}$---$f$ is a permutation-invariant set encoder, so
$\bmu_t$ depends on the set and not on the order it arrived in---and scores item $i$ by a fixed read-out
$s_i(\bmu_t)$ of the base tower. Nothing in what follows requires a distribution over $\uu$; a policy that wants one
can be built on top, which is the companion chapter's subject.

\paragraph{Requirements (formal).}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.4em,itemsep=1.5pt]
  \item[R1.] With $\mathcal E$ equal to a user's full history, $\mathrm{NDCG}@10\bigl(s(\bmu(\mathcal E))\bigr)$ is
        indistinguishable from the strongest shallow baseline on a shared harness (Def.~\ref{def:tie}), or within a stated
        and separated margin of it.
  \item[R2.] $f(\mathcal E_t)$ is defined and computable for every $t\ge 0$ with no gradient step at inference
        ($t{=}0$ yields the learned no-evidence latent), \emph{and} the admissible $\bphi_j$ are all of $\R^{d}$ rather than a
        finite set fixed when the model was fitted. The update from $\mathcal E_t$ to $\mathcal E_{t+1}$ depends on
        the new observation only through the pair $(\bphi_{t+1},y_{t+1})\in\R^{d}\times\R$, so any entity with an
        embedding is a legal member of the evidence set. The requirement is on the \emph{observation}, not on the
        number of code paths that consume it.
  \item[R3.] For a truthful answer $y_{t+1}$, $\mathbb E\bigl[\mathrm{NDCG}@10(s_{t+1})\bigr]\ge
        \mathrm{NDCG}@10(s_t)$ up to measurement noise. We treat R3 as an \emph{empirical} property to be
        demonstrated, not a theorem for a learned ranker (\S\ref{sec:limits}).
  \item[R4.] The answer value $y_j$ carries \emph{sign} and \emph{magnitude}: $y_j<0$ expresses dislike and $|y_j|$
        expresses intensity. An indicator basis has neither---a one-hot can record that an item was seen, not that
        it was disliked, nor how much.
\end{description}

\begin{definition}[Indistinguishability on a shared protocol]\label{def:tie}
A recommender is \emph{statistically indistinguishable} from a baseline on a metric if their difference's paired
per-user bootstrap confidence interval, on one shared harness (same users, items, split, metric depth), contains
zero. \textbf{This is a failure to separate, not a demonstration of equivalence.} An interval containing zero is
consistent with a small real difference in either direction that the test lacked the power to resolve, and we
never read it as evidence that two systems are equally good. A number computed on a different metric/split
(e.g.\ NDCG@10 on ML-25M vs.\ NDCG@100 on the ML-20M strong-generalisation split) is not comparable at all until
bridged (\S\ref{sec:bridge}). Indistinguishability is a claim about standing on a stated protocol; we do not read
it as a claim about absolute recommendation quality, which no offline benchmark of this kind establishes
(\S\ref{sec:limits}).
\end{definition}

\paragraph{How each requirement is checked.}
Each requirement carries exactly one verification item, R1--R4. Two integrity
controls apply across all four and exist because the per-requirement checks admit false positives
(below). Table~\ref{tab:batteryresults} states each criterion alongside what was measured against it; the definitions follow. Separately, the
\emph{ruler} itself is certified by the published-number bridge of \S\ref{sec:bridge}---that is a property
of our measuring apparatus, not of the instrument, and it is reported there.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.4em,itemsep=1.5pt]
  \item[R1 --- full strength and identity.] Evaluating the fold on the full profile reproduces the
        frozen tower's full-profile NDCG@10 (full \emph{and} tail) to within a stated, paired-bootstrapped margin---and with an
        \emph{empty} evidence set the ranking is the frozen decoder's exactly, not approximately. The identity half
        is what licenses reading every later curve as evidence about ingestion rather than about a shifted baseline.
  \item[R2 --- an open set-valued input interface.] Two halves, and only the second can fail. The set half is
        structural: the fold is defined for every $t\ge0$ with no gradient step, because it is a running sum
        over observations. The \emph{open} half is measured: a concept must be a specific direction rather than a
        popularity proxy---ranking its member items above matched non-members in the latent, and lifting the cold
        ranking beyond what the concept's own popularity explains (its top principal component partialled out)---and
        a direction obtained for a concept \emph{held out of the adapter's fit}, from its name alone, must recover
        that concept's own ranking far above chance. That last clause is the one no fixed-column model can attempt.
        We deliberately do \emph{not} require a high member-lift \emph{after} folding: the fold is trained to predict
        a user's actual targets, and a user who likes film noir does not like every noir film; an operator that
        pushed all of a concept's members up the ranking would be behaving as a genre filter, which is a failure
        mode rather than the property to gate on. The ablation of \S\ref{sec:concept:fold} measures that trade-off
        directly---the additive-union operator reaches member AUC $0.820$ but falls to $0.0991$ full
        NDCG@10, below the no-answer intercept, while the gated fold sits at $0.626$ and reaches $0.1759$. What we
        require instead is that the movement be \emph{specific}: folding a like on a concept must move that
        concept's members differently from folding a dislike on it, and differently again from folding some other
        answerable concept.
  \item[R3 --- monotonicity.] Starting cold, per-question full and tail NDCG@10 \emph{increase} to budget on a
        question set fixed in advance, so the rise comes from answer content rather than from adaptive selection.
        Judged by the curve at budget rather than every-step significance, with a canary that a single answer from
        cold already clears the no-answer intercept on both channels.
  \item[R4 --- expressive answers.] \emph{Sign}: negating every answer lowers NDCG with a CI excluding zero.
        \emph{Intensity}: neutralising values to ``indifferent'' while freezing membership costs NDCG, quality is
        monotone in the stated level. Graded input then beats a fair binarising ablation on two distinct
        comparisons, and we name what each varies: holding the fold-in support \emph{identical} and
        re-valuing each item by its star level pays $+0.0039$ full at the full profile, while \emph{adding}
        the sub-threshold answers pays far more on the short interview---$+0.0144$ at $k{=}2$---and nothing
        from $k{=}8$ onward.
\end{description}

\emph{Integrity items} (across all four):
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.4em,itemsep=1.5pt]
  \item[Existential controls.] Wrong-user, shuffled, and placebo-constant answers through the identical
        pipeline \emph{gain nothing} relative to the true-answer arm (a control may legitimately fall \emph{below}
        the no-answer intercept---a stranger's taste is worse than no information, not equivalent to it), and
        duplicating an answer changes nothing (the fold responds to information, not cardinality).
  \item[No recommender geometry in answers.] Every answer is computed from observed behaviour alone: an
        item answer is the user's own rating; a concept answer is the log watch-lift
        $\log_2\!\big((n_c+\tfrac12)/(e_c+\tfrac12)\big)$ formed from the user's watched members
        $n_c$ and from the count $e_c$ expected under their volume and the concept's catalogue popularity. Both are read from the
        user's \emph{fold-in} history; the held-out target half is excluded by construction, so an answer cannot
        encode its own target. No latent, no factor, no score and no quantity derived from the recommender enters
        an answer at any point, and the absence is asserted in code rather than assumed. The gain therefore cannot
        be the instrument recovering its own geometry, because that geometry is nowhere in the input.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Why the outcome checks alone are insufficient.}
R1 and R3 are outcome checks, and broken instruments pass both. A \emph{sign-blind} fold that
updates from only the \emph{direction} of the queried entity but never the answer \emph{value} still
reproduces the frozen tower (R1) and still beats a
random-question control---because beating random certifies that a good question was \emph{selected}, not that the
answer was \emph{ingested}; only R4's sign-flip and R3's fixed-bank arm expose it. A
\emph{circular} answer model that generates answers from the instrument's own latent geometry would inflate the R3
curve with no transferable signal. The risk is not hypothetical in this literature: evaluations commonly grant the
simulated user privileged access to the target---PEBOL's simulator answers from the ground-truth item
description~\cite{austin2024bayesian}, and unified item-and-attribute CRS work assumes the user holds and reveals
clear preferences over every attribute and item~\cite{deng2021unicorn}. The answer-provenance rule closes this by construction rather than
by measurement (\S\ref{sec:reqs}). R2, R4 and the two integrity items exist precisely to close these
outcome-check false positives.

We report \emph{both} full and tail NDCG@10 for every gate (a project reporting rule: full-only or tail-only hides the
effect, which is typically much larger on the tail than on the full catalogue). Every per-build gate has been run and passes
(Table~\ref{tab:batteryresults}); R1 is reported in full in \S\ref{sec:expdesign}.

\section{Related work: a six-family taxonomy}\label{sec:related}
% =====================================================================

We organise interview-relevant recommenders into six families---per-user retraining and meta-learning;
fold-in linear and closed-form models; amortised inference encoders; Bayesian elicitation over item space;
sequence models; and LLM-as-recommender or conversational systems---and the reading is consistent across
all of them: no family is strong on both the accuracy axis (R1) and the interview axis (R2--R4).
Figure~\ref{fig:gapmap} plots that fact quantitatively. The chapter's anchor is the per-system
\emph{master table} (Table~\ref{tab:master}), which classifies every reasonable candidate against R1--R4
\emph{and}, where one exists, prints its full/tail NDCG@10 on our shared ML-25M ruler---so that the bifurcation
is not merely asserted from published claims but is visible in the measured numbers.

\paragraph{What each table is for.} The chapter carries four. Table~\ref{tab:master} is the map: every
candidate system, what it can do (R1--R4), and how accurate it is at full profile under both protocols.
Table~\ref{tab:instrdep} is the comparison that actually decides the chapter's claim---the same systems
consuming the same short interview, where the elicitation methods are on their design point rather than off
it. Table~\ref{tab:batteryresults} is the verification battery: each requirement's criterion, what was
measured against it, and the verdict. Table~\ref{tab:freetext} is a single qualitative illustration of the
open half of R2. The published anchors that each baseline reports on its own split are \emph{not} in a
table, because they are not comparable to our ruler until bridged; they are given in \S\ref{sec:bridge},
where the bridge is run.

\paragraph{Two protocols, and why both are reported.}\label{sec:protocols} A single protocol would beg the
question, because the canonical one is not neutral between the families we are comparing.

\textsc{liang} is the canonical ruler used throughout the full-profile literature and defined
in \S\ref{sec:expdesign}: the fold-in is the user's \emph{likes} ($r>3.5$), and the candidate pool excludes
those likes. It is the protocol every published number on this split was obtained under, and it is what
makes our rows recognisable. But it discards a rating below the threshold entirely---a ``no'' does not
exist---which is exactly the signal the elicitation family is built to consume. Reporting that family only
on \textsc{liang} would therefore mean reporting it with its designed input removed.

\textsc{native} evaluates each system under the input its own design assumes, and we report the outcome
whether or not it favours that system. Each system receives \emph{its own published input contract}: binary implicit feedback for the models whose papers specify it (which is why
their two columns are identical---the input is byte-identical, and only the pool moves), and the full rated
history, signed and graded, for the ratings-native ones. To keep the comparison fair once the inputs
differ, the candidate pool then excludes \emph{every} rated fold-in item---likes and dislikes alike---for
every system identically, regardless of what that system actually consumed. That symmetry is the point: a
model shown its own dislikes cannot collect a free lift by ranking them low, because they are already out
of everyone's pool. It is also the deployment-realistic choice, since a served system filters what the user
has already rated.

The two columns are therefore not a difficulty scale, and \textsc{native} numbers are uniformly higher for a
reason that has nothing to do with capability: the larger exclusion hands back top-10 slots that a strong
personalised model was spending on items the user had already seen. Most-Popular gains $+0.028$ from this
alone and EASE $+0.066$, so the protocol systematically favours personalisation over popularity. Read each
column against its own rows, never a number in one against a number in the other.

\paragraph{One system has no native cell, and the reason is a property of the ruler rather than a gap in our
measurements.}\label{sec:nativelimit} A native cell is admissible only where constructing it does not require
optimising an objective this ruler contradicts. For every other system in the table, ``native'' names an
\emph{input} contract, and the ruler's objective touches the system only at scoring. TaNP is the exception:
its published contribution \emph{is} the training objective---episodic meta-learning for rating
regression---so the two options are not comparable. Run it as published and it is optimising rating error,
which is not the task this ruler scores; port its objective to ranking and it is no longer the published
method. There is no cell that is both faithful and comparable, so we report TaNP through its
objective-ported implicit adaptation in both columns and say why here rather than leaving a dash.

We did build and train the published version, because the alternative is asserting an incompatibility we
have not checked. Selected on its own criterion, as its paper selects, it reaches full/tail NDCG@10 of
$0.0060/0.0005$---more than an order of magnitude below the
non-personalised popularity floor of $0.1626$---while being a perfectly good rating predictor: validation
RMSE $0.696$ against $0.955$ for predicting the global mean, and a healthy latent producing $1{,}370$
distinct top-10 rankings across $2{,}000$ users. The diagnostic is the anticorrelation: over training the
loss fell from $0.862$ to $0.697$ while validation NDCG@10 drifted from $0.0111$ down to $0.0066$. \emph{The
better it became at its own objective, the worse it ranked.} Selecting adversarially on validation NDCG
instead of on its own criterion raises the number only to $0.0118$. The mechanism is the one
Cremonesi et al.~\cite{cremonesi2010performance} identified: a rating model scores quality independently of
exposure, whereas held-out likes are exposure-weighted, so it surfaces well-regarded but comparatively
unwatched films.

\emph{This says nothing about graded input.} The objective and the input change together in that run, so it
carries no evidence either way about whether reading ratings helps; that question is answerable only with the
objective held fixed, which is what the rows below do.

\paragraph{What restoring the ratings is worth, and what we cannot conclude from it.}
The premise of the \textsc{native} column is that the threshold filter withholds the signal these designs
were built for. Restoring it helps one system and hurts another, and we are unable to attribute the
difference to any property of the rating contract itself.

The clearest loss is user-kNN, measured through one estimator and one code path on two inputs: $0.3756$ on
binary co-occurrence against $0.2822$ on mean-centred ratings, a drop of $-0.0934$ full and $-0.0976$ tail.
The clearest gain is iALS with ratings as confidence~\cite{hu2008collaborative}, $c_{ui}=1+\alpha r_{ui}$,
which gains $+0.0194$ full NDCG@10 (CI $[+0.0157,+0.0231]$) over the best binary build of the same model.
Isolating that gain requires care, because the graded fold-in is $2.26\times$ richer than the likes-only
one, so a naive contrast confounds the values with the evidence volume and with the hyperparameters each
build prefers. Holding one variable at a time on the same $10{,}000$ users, every step separated in a
paired bootstrap: re-selecting the hyperparameters on the reported metric is worth $+0.0257$
(CI $[+0.0224,+0.0291]$); folding the \emph{same} additional rated items in with their values
\emph{erased} is worth $-0.0088$ (CI $[-0.0125,-0.0052]$); putting the true values back on that identical
support recovers $+0.0282$ (CI $[+0.0261,+0.0302]$). More evidence, recorded as though every rating were a
like, makes the model worse; the values, not the additional evidence, carry the gain. We use that fixed-support step to decompose and
never as a headline, because a control that mislabels dislikes as likes is not the baseline any deployed
positive-only system presents.

The largest loss on record turns out to be configuration rather than anything about ratings. RBMF's graded
arm was recorded at $-0.070$ against its binary build; re-selecting each arm's ridge on validation and
holding one frozen basis, the same comparison is $-0.0045$ full (CI $[-0.0069,-0.0022]$) and $+0.0046$
tail (CI $[+0.0021,+0.0071]$)---both intervals exclude zero, so the effect is real on each axis and points
in opposite directions, but the magnitude is a twentieth of what was reported. The decomposition says
where the old number came from: mean-centring the targets costs $0.0981$ full on its own, and folding the
sub-threshold items in with their values erased costs a further $0.0499$, while restoring the true values
on that identical support is worth $+0.0454$ (CI $[+0.0428,+0.0479]$). The published centring, not the
rating contract, accounts for the loss reported previously.

This is a general point about the column rather than a fact about one system. Three of the ratings-native rows---iALS, RBMF and the conjugate belief---
shared one hyperparameter setting, selected by a validation sweep that optimised NDCG@100 on a previous
dataset while this chapter reports NDCG@10. Re-selecting on the reported metric moves RBMF by $+0.0601$
full in this column, iALS by $+0.0281$ and belief-MF by $+0.0267$, and RBMF's own regularisation---not the
shared basis---accounts for most of its gain. User-kNN's loss likewise has a specific cause rather than a
general one: mean-centring is applied to the value \emph{and} to the cosine basis, so the centred build
differs from its binary twin in two ways at once. We therefore claim only what we measured. On this ruler,
restoring the published rating contract does not reliably improve these systems, the one system it clearly
helps uses ratings as confidence in a plain factorisation, and we make no claim about why.

The consequence for how this chapter argues is worth being explicit about. We are \emph{not} entitled to
say that the canonical binarisation handicaps the elicitation family at full profile: user-kNN is much
worse on its own ratings, RBMF is within half a point either way, and the one system that gains
clearly, iALS, is not an elicitation method at all. Nor are we entitled to present graded input as something only the instrument
turns to account---iALS is a second system that gains from values, and it is a plain matrix factorisation.

Against the appropriate baseline---a positive-only system, which \emph{drops} sub-threshold answers rather than
mislabelling them---iALS gains more from ratings at full profile than the instrument does: $+0.0194$
against $+0.0022$ (CI $[+0.0008,+0.0036]$). Our full-profile standing does not rest on graded input, and
the tower is initialised from a binary-trained recommender. What the levels buy the instrument is
concentrated in the interview, where they are worth $+0.0102$ at $k{=}2$ and nothing from $k{=}8$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:gradedcurve}).

One comparison must be kept out of that sentence. Re-valuing a fixed revealed set against a control that
records \emph{every} answered item as a like is worth far more---$+0.1032$ for the instrument, $+0.0282$
for iALS---but that control mislabels dislikes rather than discarding them, so its margin grows with
interview length simply because more revealed items means more corrupted ones. It is the right rung for
\emph{decomposing} where a rating acts, which is how the confidence ladder above uses it, and the wrong
number for claiming what graded input is worth. We report it only in the former role.

What the sub-threshold signal is worth to \emph{us} is a question about the \emph{interview}, where evidence
is scarce and a single ``no'' is a large fraction of what the model knows (\S\ref{sec:batteryresults}). The
\textsc{native} column's value is therefore that it forecloses an objection rather than that it changes an
ordering: it removes any doubt that these systems scored below the instrument only because the protocol
had withheld the input their designs assume. One row now also shows what the column was built to detect---a
system that reads its own contract and improves---which is what makes the other rows' losses attributable
to those designs rather than to the protocol.

\paragraph{No dash is an unexplained omission.} Every unmeasured row carries one of three dispositions. (Systems with neither a measurable number nor a distinct capability profile---e.g.\ SLIM, strictly dominated by its closed-form successor EASE in every published comparison---are cited in the text but given no row.) \emph{Not run here}: GF-CF, BSPM, BERT4Rec and EDDI have public code and are runnable on this ruler, but carry no number in this chapter; the graph-filter family is represented by the measured Turbo-CF row and the sequence family by the measured SASRec row. \emph{Best-effort measured}: for systems whose full-profile core is runnable but whose exact recipe is unpublished or benchmark-bound, we report a best-effort replication of that core with every substitution stated---the node recommender that is the Golbandi tree's core, at $0.3065/0.1895$, and the RBMF/Functional-MF seed at $0.2915/0.2071$. \textbf{One class of adaptation is worth naming.} Three rows are ratings-native by publication---Golbandi's
interview tree routes users by \emph{lovers, haters and unknowns} and is evaluated on RMSE,
RBMF/Functional-MF is a rating regression, and TaNP is episodic meta-learning for rating prediction---while
the canonical protocol is implicit and binary (\S\ref{sec:bridge}). We adapt their mechanisms to the
implicit contract rather than importing their task, which is what Liang et al.\ do for the ratings-native
baselines in the study that defines this split~\cite{liang2018variational}. Under \textsc{liang} every row
therefore sees the same information: the same binarised fold-in, the same candidate pool, the same binary
targets. The second column exists precisely to relax the first of those---each system reads the input its
own publication assumes, while the pool and the targets stay fixed for everyone---so that a low score
cannot be attributed to a representation the system was never designed to consume. Numbers obtained under
a contract we have not run are not comparable to either column in either direction, so we
report only on-ruler values and do not claim that a row would be higher or lower under its original task.
The same caution applies to ICF, whose ceiling we take from the measured iALS row: ICF is Bayesian
probabilistic MF over ratings, while iALS is implicit-with-confidence, so that substitution crosses an
input regime as well as a model family. For B\i y\i k and ConTS we print a dash rather than a number, because neither reports full-catalogue accuracy at all---B\i y\i k's NDCG is agreement between a user's true and estimated top-$|S|$ items over 16 sampled users on slates of 5---and measuring our own reconstruction of a mechanism they share would produce a number that is ours, not theirs. That mechanism is measured in \S\ref{sec:method}, where it belongs: as the \emph{rejected alternative} against which our learned fold is justified---ranking on a closed-form accumulator's accumulated mean scores $0.2267/0.1300$ where the learned fold reaches $0.3496/0.2491$, so uniformity costs roughly $0.12$ full NDCG. We do not maintain such an accumulator; we measured one to establish what forgoing it is worth. The elicitation corner is blank in this column because full-catalogue top-$N$ accuracy is not what these papers report---and that is a defect in the record rather than a neutral choice of emphasis, for the reason set out in \S\ref{sec:gap:why}. \emph{Not runnable by construction}: PEBOL maintains per-item Beta posteriors over an LLM-scored shortlist of $\sim$100 items and contains no full-catalogue ranker to run; GATE elicits but does not rank; zero-shot LLM ranking of an 18k catalogue is neither computationally nor methodologically meaningful at full-profile scale (and its collaborative weakness is already documented~\cite{he2023large}); BCIE requires its authors' knowledge graph, and a port would substitute so much that the number would no longer be theirs---its conjugate-fold core is covered by the B\i y\i k-style best-effort arm. ICF is the one row whose full-profile core is \emph{already} measured: its underlying model is Bayesian probabilistic MF, so its full-profile ceiling is the measured iALS row ($0.2703$).

\paragraph{The measured-accuracy column falls across the elicitation corner.}
Table~\ref{tab:master}, read down its measured-accuracy column, divides cleanly. Every \emph{competitive} number---a full/tail
NDCG@10 at or near the frontier on our shared ML-25M ruler---sits in block (a) or (b): the item-only linear and
amortised recommenders (RecVAE $0.3540$, EASE $0.3476$, EDLAE $0.3433$). The elicitation corner, block (c), is either
\emph{blank} or \emph{measurably weak}. Both of its runnable cores land well below the frontier---the Golbandi node
recommender at $0.3065/0.1895$ and the RBMF/Functional-MF seed at $0.2915/0.2071$---and both carry an \xmark\ on R1.
The rest of the corner---EDDI, BCIE, UNICORN, PEBOL, GATE, the LLM-zero-shot CRS---has a blank accuracy column
entirely. This is not an accident of our harness. Most CRS and elicitation papers never report full-catalogue
top-N accuracy at all (they report elicitation RMSE, cold-start MRR/MAP on a shortlist, or critique metrics), and
several \emph{cannot produce it by construction}: a per-item Beta posterior (PEBOL) or the decision tree itself
(Golbandi, whose node recommender we measure only as a best-effort core) does not induce a ranking over the whole
catalogue, and an LLM CRS needs an API call per candidate to score one. Conversely, every system at the frontier is
item-only on a one-hot basis---\xmark\ on R2 and R4. The two blocks are disjoint: no row combines a frontier
accuracy number with \cmark s across R2--R4. The top-right cell of the
gap map---all four requirements met \emph{and} a competitive full-profile number---is empty across the published
literature, and is occupied by the instrument this chapter builds. That is the chapter's thesis, and the master
table is where it is most visible.

\paragraph{The accuracy corner (families ii--iii).}
On the canonical ML-20M strong-generalisation split (10k validation / 10k test held-out users, NDCG@100), the
full-profile frontier has been \emph{flat since 2019--2020} and is held by shallow models, not deep nets:
EASE~\cite{steck2019ease} at NDCG@100 $0.420$, Mult-VAE~\cite{liang2018variational} at $0.426$,
RecVAE~\cite{ren2020recvae} at $0.442$ (top of this split), with EDLAE~\cite{steck2020edlae} in the same class; SLIM
($0.401$) and WMF/iALS ($0.386$) are the classic floors. Dacrema et al.~\cite{dacrema2019progress} is the binding
warning: most deeper ``SOTA'' gains vanish on this exact split. The graph-filter leaders
(GF-CF~\cite{shen2021gfcf}, BSPM~\cite{choi2023bspm}, Turbo-CF~\cite{park2024turbocf}) are the 2023--24 speed$+$accuracy
front on Gowalla/Yelp/Amazon but do not report this ML-20M split, so they are admissible to our accuracy tier only if
re-run on our harness. Turbo-CF is, and lands at $0.2622/0.1621$---mid-pack rather than frontier at this catalogue size. All of family (ii)/(iii) are item-only: their input is a one-hot (or set-indicator) interaction
vector, which \emph{cannot encode} a signed value, an out-of-catalogue concept, or a continuous direction---R2 and R4 are an
architecture property here, not a tuning gap.

\paragraph{The elicitation corner (family iv, and vi).}
B\i y\i k et al.~\cite{biyik2023soft} maintain a multivariate-Gaussian belief over a user embedding and treat both item
comparisons and ``soft attributes'' (e.g.\ ``scarier'') as directions (concept-activation vectors) in the item space,
selecting queries by expected value of information---the nearest thing to R2$+$R3$+$R4 together.
ConTS~\cite{li2021seamlessly} unifies items and attributes as Thompson-sampling arms with a posterior over a cold-start
user. BCIE~\cite{toroghi2023bcie} folds critiques into a \emph{frozen} factorisation by exact conjugate-Gaussian
updates---the closest published analogue of our frozen-tower fold. PEBOL~\cite{austin2024bayesian} keeps a per-item Beta
posterior updated by LLM-extracted aspects and NLI scoring, reporting cold-start MRR@10 $0.27$ versus a $0.17$ baseline.
None of these reports full-profile accuracy at the EASE/RecVAE bar; each is a belief layer, not a certified tower.

\paragraph{The set-encoder and cold-start-NP precedents (families i, iii).}
EDDI/Partial-VAE~\cite{ma2019eddi} is the permutation-invariant set-encoder over an arbitrary observed subset with
information-gain acquisition---exactly R2's architecture, but demonstrated outside recommendation and without a full-profile
CF result or an attribute/concept channel. TaNP~\cite{lin2021tanp} gives a neural-process, stochastic-process
uncertainty over a value-carrying set input for cold-start---the R2$+$R4 shape, but not at the full-profile frontier nor
channel-agnostic. These are cited as the mechanism precedents our design instantiates, not as rivals for the
conjunction.

\paragraph{The gap map.}
Figure~\ref{fig:gapmap} places every system on two axes: \emph{interview-compatibility} on the $x$-axis---a coarse
ordinal capability count, the number of \textbf{R2--R4} marks a system satisfies in the master table
(R1 is excluded because accuracy is the $y$-axis)---and \emph{measured full-profile NDCG@10 on our
ML-25M ruler} (R1) on the $y$-axis. The population is bimodal: the accuracy corner collapses onto $x{=}0$, the
interview-capable systems sit at $x\ge1$ with weak or non-existent full-profile accuracy, no published system
exceeds $x{=}2$, and no system bridges---the top-right, high compatibility \emph{and} frontier accuracy, is
empty.

\section{Gap analysis}\label{sec:gap}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{The verdict.}
No single published system satisfies all of $\{$R1 frontier-parity full-profile $\wedge$ R2 any-length set $\wedge$
R3 monotone $\wedge$ R4 expressive answers$\}$. The binding
near-miss is B\i y\i k et al.~\cite{biyik2023soft}: it fails R1 (a $d{=}50$ ALS two-tower rather than a
certified RecVAE-class tower, and it reports no full-catalogue accuracy---its NDCG is computed between a user's true
top-$|S|$ items and the top-$|S|$ estimated from the posterior, over 16 sampled test users on slates of 5, an
in-simulator alignment measure) and gives only an approximate, ``generally not Gaussian'' posterior; it fails R2 in
the specific sense of \S\ref{sec:gap} (a per-concept supervised probe over a closed tag list, not an open
vocabulary). Every other candidate fails R3 or R4 outright, and we are aware of no system that occupies the
conjunction.

\paragraph{Where each near-miss falls short.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{B\i y\i k et al.~2023}~\cite{biyik2023soft} --- holds \emph{part} of R2 (items and soft attributes
        both enter as directions), over a \emph{frozen} item tower---they train the CF model
        and the attribute directions separately, as we do. \emph{Fails R1}: a $d{=}50$ ALS two-tower, and accuracy is
        reported on their own elicitation task (in-simulator top-$|S|$ agreement over 16 users, slates of 5), not the
        EASE/RecVAE bar. \emph{Fails R2} in the sense that decides it: the directions are \emph{per-attribute supervised
        probes} over an enumerated tag list---each a logistic regressor fitted from labelled positive/negative items,
        trained for the 164 most-frequent tags---so the vocabulary is fixed before the user arrives, which is exactly
        what R2 forbids. R4 is approximate (sampled EVOI over a non-Gaussian posterior). This is the closest prior system, and
        the separation is exactly (a) an \emph{open} vocabulary through one global adapter with no per-concept supervision,
        (b) the R1 accuracy bar on a shared full-catalogue ruler, (c) exact conjugacy vs.\ approximate posterior.
  \item \textbf{ConTS}~\cite{li2021seamlessly} --- R3 $+$ a unified item/attribute action space.
        \emph{Fails R2} (a fixed categorical attribute schema; no arbitrary embedding, no continuous or open token) and
        \emph{R1} (a bandit over arms, not a full-profile encoder).
  \item \textbf{EDDI / Partial-VAE}~\cite{ma2019eddi} --- R3 (information-gain over a belief) $+$ set input.
        \emph{Fails R1} (not a recommendation model / no full-catalogue CF ranking) and \emph{R2} (a fixed feature set; no attribute or
        concept channel as demonstrated).
  \item \textbf{RecVAE / EASE}~\cite{ren2020recvae,steck2019ease} --- \emph{R1 yes.} \emph{Fail R2} (an item-only
        one-hot / indicator basis admits no concept and no continuous direction), \emph{R4} (nor a signed or graded
        value), and \emph{R3} (point estimate; RecVAE's latent posterior is item-only and is not used as an actionable
        belief).
  \item \textbf{PEBOL}~\cite{austin2024bayesian} --- R3 (per-item Beta) $+$ R2 (LLM-extracted aspects, an open
        vocabulary) $+$ strong cold-start.
        \emph{Fails R1} (no full-profile CF ranking) and its belief is \emph{per item}, not a shared user covariance; no
        monotonicity guarantee, and a yes/no answer carries no intensity (R4).
  \item \textbf{SASRec / BERT4Rec}~\cite{kang2018sasrec,sun2019bert4rec} --- re-encode the sequence at inference.
        \emph{Fail R2, R3 and R4} (item-only, order-sensitive, point estimate, non-monotone---a later token can
        reorder everything). Token input is trivially satisfied but is \emph{not} value-carrying or mixed-type, so
        ``interview $=$ tokens'' does not hold; and BERT4Rec's fold-in advantage carries a replicability
        caveat~\cite{petrov2022replicability}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The three legs an item-indicator basis cannot carry.}
Three input channels have no faithful encoding in the item-indicator basis of every R1-bar model: (i)
graded/signed per-entity values (the basis has no sign and no magnitude---a ``dislike'' is not representable as a
negative one-hot without changing the likelihood), (ii) concepts whose vocabulary is not fixed when the model is
fitted (a column exists only for an entity present at fit time), and (iii) arbitrary continuous direction tokens.
Folding these as \emph{linear observations in a frozen latent} is the one leg of the conjunction we find nowhere in
the literature.

\emph{We should be precise about which of these is impossible and which is merely lossy}, because one obvious
reduction exists and we do not want to overstate. A concept \emph{all of whose members are already in the
catalogue} can be approximated by a weighted indicator over those members, and that vector can be fed to EASE or
RecVAE without adding any column at all.

That reduction is nonetheless bounded in two ways, and the first is the one that matters, because it applies to
\emph{every} concept rather than to an exotic subset. A nonnegative indicator over member items has no encoding
for a negative answer: ``I dislike westerns'' is not expressible as a weighted bag of westerns, whatever the
weights, because the basis carries no sign. The same limit is what the critiquing literature runs into---across
eight systems we find the critique is a one-hot indicator over a closed set, without exception (\S\ref{sec:related})---and it is
what our own sign contrast measures directly ($+0.2096$, CI $[+0.1986,+0.2208]$). Signed concept answers are
therefore inexpressible in the item basis for in-catalogue and out-of-catalogue concepts alike.

The second bound is absolute rather than lossy, and applies to a narrower set: for a concept coined after the
model was fitted, for a phrase with no curated counterpart, or for a direction a policy emits, there are no
member columns to weight, so no reduction exists at any fidelity. The two together are why we describe the legs
as having no faithful encoding rather than as untuned: sign fails universally, and expandability fails
absolutely.

The distinction that matters is between an \emph{open} and a \emph{closed} channel. An item-indicator model can be
extended with a concept---as a pseudo-item column, or an extra input unit---but only by adding a column, and a column
is fixed when the model is fitted (\S\ref{sec:expdesign}). Its concept vocabulary is therefore frozen at training
time. Our interface takes a direction $\bphi\in\R^{d}$ and a value, so any entity with an embedding is a legal
observation at inference: a paraphrase, a concept coined after deployment, or a continuous direction emitted by a
policy and snapped to a nearby meaningful axis. This is what we mean by channel-agnostic, and it is a property of the
interface rather than a quantity to be tuned.

\subsection{Feasibility and necessity of the conjunction}\label{sec:gap:why}

One objection to the analysis above is that the conjunction is inexpensive to satisfy: take a frontier
recommender, carry a conjugate belief beside it, and the grid fills in. Three considerations tell against
this.

\emph{Most of the frontier has nothing to carry a belief over.} A belief over a user latent needs two
things to be well posed: a user vector in a dense latent, and a direction in that latent to serve as a
meaningful query. EASE has neither: its parameters are an item--item weight matrix and its ``user'' is the
raw interaction row, one coordinate per catalogue item. There is no low-dimensional taste vector to be
uncertain about and no direction to read $w^\top\bSigma w$ along. The same holds for EDLAE and for the
training-free graph filters. For the closed-form family---most of the accuracy corner---the construction is
not difficult, it is undefined. It is available to the amortised-encoder family, and that is where we build
it.

\emph{Where it is available, it carries a cost.} Such a layer has to leave full-profile accuracy exactly intact,
which is a property to be measured and not assumed: our empty interview reproduces the frozen decoder's
ranking identically (Spearman $1.0000$, $|\bmu_0|=0$), and the obvious alternative---ranking on the
conjugate mean---forfeits $0.15$ full NDCG (\S\ref{sec:method}). Nor does a dense latent accept concepts
merely because it is dense: a raw concept centroid ranks its own member items at AUC $0.44$, \emph{below
chance}, and only whitening makes it a direction ($0.94$). The channel is built, not inherited.

\emph{And the reason to pay is that policy conclusions are not portable across recommenders.} This is the
argument for an instrument, and it is empirical rather than rhetorical. On a strong ingestion model,
differences between question-selection strategies compress: a \emph{random} question from an answerable
bank matches popularity-ranked items at $q{=}1,2,4,8$ and loses only at $q{=}16$; a one-level adaptive
probe pays $+0.0015$ full (CI $[+0.0007,+0.0024]$); and an uncertainty-guided selector beats random by
$+0.0036$ at the largest budget alone. A weaker recommender leaves more of the work to the question, so the same strategy
gap measured there is larger---and, if strategies are affected unequally, need not even preserve the
\emph{ordering}. A policy result obtained on a weak recommender therefore carries an unknown amount of its
own substrate, which is exactly the confound this chapter exists to remove.

\paragraph{On what the elicitation literature reports.} We are direct about this because it bears on the
table above. Reporting cold-start MRR over a hundred-item shortlist, or top-$|S|$ agreement between a
simulated user and a posterior, or turn counts to a success state, measures a system on a task that is not
the one it would perform in service. Full-catalogue NDCG is not a formality: it is what a user experiences
when a deployed system ranks the whole catalogue for them after an interview, and it is the only number in
which an elicitation gain and a recommender weakness can be compared on the same scale. A shortlist metric
can look excellent while the underlying model could not rank the catalogue at all, and a policy improvement
measured on it may not survive contact with $18{,}359$ candidates. We therefore treat the blank accuracy
column not as a difference of emphasis but as missing evidence, and we report full \emph{and} tail
NDCG@10 for every arm of our own work, including the arms where it is unflattering.

\paragraph{Prior art, cited and not claimed.}
We must credit, and do not claim priority over: attribute-answers-as-directions plus a Bayesian
fold~\cite{biyik2023soft}; unified item$+$attribute belief and value of information~\cite{biyik2023soft,li2021seamlessly};
conjugate-Gaussian folds over a frozen factorisation~\cite{toroghi2023bcie}; permutation-invariant set-encoders with
information-gain acquisition~\cite{ma2019eddi}; cold-start recommenders with value-carrying set input~\cite{lin2021tanp};
the principle that added information does not degrade optimal
decisions~\cite{good1967principle,blackwell1953equivalent}; and the now-crowded ``uncertainty-native
recommender'' area~\cite{wang2025bdecf}. Our defensible novelty is only the \emph{conjunction} of all four requirements
in one frozen certified tower, plus the three unencodable legs and the acceptance battery that certifies them.

% =====================================================================
\section{The instrument}\label{sec:method}
% =====================================================================

The design follows directly from the gap analysis: use the shallow frozen tower that already holds the accuracy
corner (R1, and the flat-since-2019 frontier means no deep-net detour is warranted), and add over its frozen latent
a permutation-invariant set encoder that folds an evidence set of any length to a point (R2, R3), with every
channel entering as a direction paired with a value (R4). The tower half is built and certified
(\S\ref{sec:expdesign}); the fold is derived here, fitted on real data, and gated in
\S\ref{sec:batteryresults}.

\subsection{Frozen shallow frontier tower}
The substrate is a certified shallow recommender---a RecVAE-class amortised encoder or a closed-form
autoencoder~\cite{ren2020recvae,steck2019ease}---trained once on the full catalogue and then \emph{frozen}. It exposes
(a) item factor rows $\Q_i\in\R^{d}$, (b) a decoder read-out $s_i(\uu)=\Q_i^\top\uu + \beta_i$ with a learned bias
$\beta_i$, and (c) the encoder that maps a full interaction set to a latent. Freezing is the guarantee that R1 (full strength preserved) is a property of the substrate, not of the
fold placed over it. It is a design choice we share with the soft-attribute line rather than one that distinguishes us:
G\"opfert et al. are explicit that ``we do not use the tag data when training the collaborative filtering
model''~\cite{gopfert2024cav}, and B\i y\i k et al. train the CF model and the attribute directions
separately~\cite{biyik2023soft}. We cite that convergence as independent support for the frozen-substrate design. We deliberately do \emph{not} extend the linear basis with concept columns
(that stays in the item-indicator world and fails R2, because the vocabulary would again be fixed when the
model is fitted), nor retrain the tower to accommodate a channel, which would put R1 back at risk for no
stated gain. The tower is now \emph{selected and certified}: the graded-native set-encoder T2$'$, trained
once in its pre-registered configuration, scores $0.3496/0.2491$ full/tail on the held-out test cohort---indistinguishable from
the $0.3540/0.2497$ RecVAE bar on the tail, and $1.2\%$ short of it on the full profile---and reproduces the frozen decoder exactly at the empty
interview (\S\ref{sec:expdesign}). It is this checkpoint that every channel below is folded into.

\subsection{Channel-agnostic tokens: one representation, one form of observation}
Every channel supplies the same kind of thing: an observation is a pair
\begin{equation}\label{eq:obs}
  (\bphi_j,\,y_j)\ \in\ \R^{d}\times\R,
\end{equation}
a direction $\bphi_j$ in the frozen latent and a value $y_j$, and it is this that makes the channel set open (R2).
Two folds consume those pairs in the system we
report: the tower's own set encoder for item evidence, and a trained gated operator for concept evidence
(\S\ref{sec:concept:fold}). We are explicit that these are distinct operators rather than one code path, because the
unification that matters is of the representation: any entity with an embedding is expressible as an observation,
which is what no item-indicator model can say. Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} summarises the interface.

\paragraph{Why a learned fold rather than a closed-form one.} A closed-form conjugate accumulator over the frozen
factorisation is itself a legal ranker---score item $i$ by $\Q_i^\top\bmu_t+\beta_i$---and it would make one
uniform fold serve every channel. We measured that option rather than assuming it: ranked by its accumulated mean
it reaches full/tail NDCG@10 $0.2267/0.1300$ on our ruler, against $0.3496/0.2491$ for the learned fold
(Table~\ref{tab:master}). Uniformity therefore costs roughly $0.12$ full NDCG---it forfeits R1---which is why the
instrument folds with a learned set encoder and pays for the extra operator.

The channels, then, are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1.5pt]
  \item \textbf{Items.} $\bphi_j=\Q_i$ (the frozen item factor). A graded or signed answer sets $y_j$; a dislike is a
        negative $y_j$ along the same direction---representable here precisely because the observation model
        \eqref{eq:obs} has a sign and a magnitude, unlike a one-hot indicator.
  \item \textbf{Out-of-catalogue concepts.} $\bphi_j$ is a concept direction in the latent (e.g.\ a centroid of member
        factors, or a text-adapter image of the concept), folded exactly like an item---no catalogue column required.
  \item \textbf{Entities / free text.} An embedding is projected into the latent by a fixed adapter and folded
        identically.
  \item \textbf{Continuous direction tokens.} A policy may emit any $\bphi_j\in\R^{d}$ directly (the continuous-action
        interface the companion chapter uses); the fold does not distinguish it from a catalogue entity.
\end{itemize}
The signed/graded, out-of-catalogue, and continuous legs are the three channels the R1-bar basis cannot carry
(\S\ref{sec:gap}).

\paragraph{Any direction is a legal observation: an illustration.} The signed/graded leg is delivered and
measured (R4, Figure~\ref{fig:gradedcurve}). The concept leg is delivered over the genome vocabulary.
The third leg---an \emph{arbitrary} direction, including one for an entity that has no catalogue column and
was never seen in training---is a property of the interface rather than of any particular channel:
everything downstream of Eq.~\eqref{eq:obs} consumes a pair $(\bphi,y)$, so any entity with an
embedding is admissible. We illustrate that concretely.

Each of the $1{,}031$ genome concepts has a direction in the latent and a name in English, so those pairs
determine a single global linear map from a frozen general-purpose sentence encoder into the frozen latent,
in closed form. We fit it on a random $80\%$ of the concepts and ask what happens to the $20\%$ it never
saw: for a held-out concept, the direction predicted \emph{from its name alone} recovers the top of that
concept's own item ranking more than $300\times$ more often than chance ($0.167$ top-10 overlap against a
$0.0005$ random baseline; cosine $0.638$ to the true direction). No per-concept probe is fitted, no inventory is
consulted, and nothing is retrained---a phrase becomes a direction by one matrix multiplication.

Because the map is defined on any string, it also accepts phrases that are not in our concept vocabulary.
To make that meaningful rather than decorative, each phrase below is screened against the vocabulary two
ways---no genome tag occurs as a whole-word substring of it, and no concept sits within cosine $0.6$ of
it---and we print the \emph{closest} concept we actually hold, so the reader can see how far the phrase
sits from anything curated. (The screen is not cosmetic: it rejects ``revenge served slowly'',
``loneliness in a big city'' and ``a detective who drinks too much'', because \emph{revenge},
\emph{loneliness} and \emph{detective} are all themselves genome tags.)

We present this as an \emph{illustration of what the interface permits}, not as a contribution to mapping
language into collaborative latents---a question with an established literature of its own, in which
attribute directions are obtained either by per-concept supervised probes~\cite{gopfert2024cav,biyik2023soft}
or by per-attribute corpus retrieval~\cite{balog2021softattr}. Fitting such a map \emph{well} is a research
direction this architecture opens rather than one this chapter pursues: text underdetermines collaborative
taste, and how much of that gap a better-fitted adapter can close is exactly the kind of question the
instrument now makes measurable.

\paragraph{The observation is independent of its provenance.} One consequence is worth naming because
it requires no additional mechanism. The discussion above treats a phrase as something the \emph{system} elected to ask
about. But Eq.~\eqref{eq:obs} reads a pair $(\bphi,y)$ and is indifferent to its provenance: a user who
volunteers ``I'm in the mood for something about grief'' has supplied a direction and a positive value,
which folds exactly as an answer to a question we posed would. The same holds for a critique after a
recommendation (``less bleak than that'') and for a direction a continuous policy emits and never renders
as language at all. An item-indicator model cannot accept any of these, because none has a column; a
model with a curated attribute list can accept only those a curator anticipated. A user-initiated request
is thus not a separate feature to be built on top of this instrument---it is the same fold read in the
other direction, and the reason it is available is R2. We have not evaluated proactive requests here, and
the quality of any such interaction would rest on the text adapter whose limits are set out above.

\section{The concept channel: derivation, lineage, and deployment currency}\label{sec:concept}
% =====================================================================

The out-of-catalogue concept leg is the most demanding of the three---it is the one with no
catalogue column, no interaction count, and no sign in the item-indicator basis---so we work it out in full. Three
things must be made concrete: how a concept answer becomes an observation pair $(\bphi_c, y_c)$ for the fold
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:obs}; where each piece sits in the published record (so that we claim only the conjunction, never
a component); and why this channel is the one that forces the battery to gate what a \emph{deployed} interview
elicits and not only what the operator can express. This section
is written at derivation level; the concept-channel numbers below are measured on the internal ML-25M interview
harness and are reported as such.

\subsection{A signed, exposure-corrected affinity estimator}\label{sec:concept:est}

A concept answer must supply the value $y_c$ that Eq.~\eqref{eq:obs} reads along the concept direction. We want that
value to be \emph{signed} (a user can dislike ``horror,'' not merely fail to like it), \emph{popularity-corrected} (a
concept made of blockbusters must not read as universal affinity), \emph{volume-corrected} (a heavy watcher must not
read as liking everything), and \emph{confidence-bearing} (a concept the user has plausibly never been exposed to is a
\emph{refusal}, not a dislike). Two estimators, an implicit and an explicit one, combine to meet this.

Let $\mathcal I_u$ be the user's watched set, $p_i$ the catalogue marginal of item $i$, and let concept $c$ have member
set $M_c$. Write $n_c=|\mathcal I_u\cap M_c|$ for the user's watched members and $e_c=|\mathcal I_u|\cdot\pi_c$ for the
number \emph{expected} under the user's volume times the concept's popularity $\pi_c=\sum_{i\in M_c}p_i$. The implicit
\emph{selection} estimator is the add-$\tfrac12$ log watch-lift
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sel}
  \mathrm{SEL}(u,c) \;=\; \log_2\frac{n_c+0.5}{e_c+0.5}
  \;\;\approx\;\; \mathrm{PMI}(u,c),
\end{equation}
which is precisely the \emph{pointwise mutual information} between the user and the concept: the denominator's $\pi_c$
divides out concept popularity, and forming $e_c$ from the user's own volume divides out user activity, so SEL is
positive for genuine affinity, near zero for popularity-explained co-occurrence, and \emph{negative for avoidance}.
Its normalised form, $\mathrm{NPMI}=\mathrm{PMI}/(-\log_2 \hat p_{uc})\in[-1,1]$, tames PMI's
small-count inflation and yields a bounded, comparable score. The explicit \emph{value} estimator is a support-shrunk
signed rating residual,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:val}
  \mathrm{VAL}(u,c) \;=\; \frac{n_c}{n_c+\lambda}\;\bar r^{\,\mathrm{res}}_{uc},
  \qquad \lambda=3,\qquad
  \bar r^{\,\mathrm{res}}_{uc}=\frac{1}{n_c}\!\!\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_u\cap M_c}\!\!\bigl(r_{ui}-\bar r_i\bigr),
\end{equation}
the mean rating the user gives the concept's members \emph{above each item's own mean}, shrunk toward zero by the
Beta-style factor $n_c/(n_c+\lambda)$ so that a one-film opinion counts for little. Both estimators are signed and
combine into a four-band answer: a \emph{graded like} (SEL/VAL band above the neutral zone), a \emph{neutral}
(indistinguishable-from-exposure), a \emph{graded dislike} (band below), and a \emph{refusal} issued exactly when the
expected support falls below a threshold, $e_c<\tau$, i.e.\ when the user has plausibly never been exposed to the
concept and no answer should be forced.

\paragraph{Lineage.} Every piece is established prior art. The log-lift
\eqref{eq:sel} is PMI/NPMI, the standard popularity-corrected association score. Reading a \emph{magnitude} of implicit
consumption as evidence is Hu--Koren--Volinsky's implicit-confidence template~\cite{hu2008collaborative}---except that
HKV lets the count re-enter \emph{only as a loss confidence weight, never as a sign}, which is exactly the assumption
our signed estimator breaks. The principled account of why a non-watch is not a dislike is
ExpoMF~\cite{liang2016expomf}: it separates a latent \emph{exposure} variable (was the user even aware of the item)
from \emph{preference}, so that non-consumption is modelled as ``not exposed'' \emph{or} ``exposed-and-disliked''---the
generative gold standard our refusal band ($e_c<\tau$) and popularity-corrected $e_c$ approximate cheaply and in closed
form. The unbiased-learning route to the same correction is attribute-level exposure
propensity~\cite{qin2020attributepropensity}, which IPS-reweights implicit feedback by a per-attribute propensity;
it is the heavier, variance-prone alternative to our moment estimator. Finally, calibrated
recommendation~\cite{steck2018calibrated} builds a user's normalised genre \emph{distribution} $p(g\mid u)$---the
closest widely-used ``concept profile,'' but a distribution that sums to one and therefore encodes relative emphasis
only: it \emph{cannot express dislike}, which is precisely the expressive gap SEL$<0$ fills.

\paragraph{Why the implicit channel, quantified.} On an informativeness decomposition (a per-concept linear fit of the
oracle concept affinity onto each candidate answer, $R^2$), the implicit watch-lift dominates: SEL explains $0.376$ of
the affinity variance, the explicit rating residual VAL a further $+0.042$ (SEL$+$VAL $0.398$), while an LLM-elicited
ordinal answer explains only $0.017$ (an internal decomposition)---implicit outperforms the explicit LLM answer by
roughly $22\times$.
Because SEL is built from raw watch counts and item marginals, it uses no recommender geometry, so this signal is
\emph{non-circular by construction} (it even beats a model-geometry projection of the same residuals). This is the
evidence that the concept channel's value is worth carrying signed rather than as a mere membership indicator; the
decomposition is an informativeness measurement, not an interview curve, and is reported as such.

\subsection{The fold operator: whitened directions and a gated fold-to-point}\label{sec:concept:fold}

The direction $\bphi_c$ must point where the concept's taste lives, not where popularity lives. A raw member centroid
fails this: a shared popularity component (random concept pairs have cosine $\approx 0.5$) masks the genre geometry.
Removing it---centre the member factors and strip the top-1 principal component (the popularity axis), the
all-but-the-top whitening of Mu and Viswanath~\cite{mu2018allbutthetop}---turns the centroid into a usable direction: concept-membership
discrimination through the frozen decoder rises from AUC $0.44$ (raw, at chance) to $0.94$ (whitened). We therefore
take $\bphi_c$ to be the \emph{whitened member centroid}, IDF-weighted by $1/\log(1+|M_c|)$ so broad genres do not
dominate. Whitening is a fix, not a contribution, and is cited as such.

The operator that consumes $(\bphi_c,y_c)$ is where the design decision lives, and the two candidate families
separate cleanly under ablation. An \emph{additive-union} operator scores each item by summing concept contributions on
a popularity floor, $s_i=\beta_i+\sum_c w_c\,\langle \W_d\Q_i,\bphi_c\rangle$. It has the right monotonicity floor (an
empty interview scores exactly $\beta_i$) but the wrong composition: it scores the \emph{union} of directions, so
correlated sibling concepts (``dark,'' ``gritty,'' ``noir'') re-add the same acclaim axis and over-count. Measured
against a no-answer intercept of $0.1279$ full NDCG@10, the additive channel peaks early (about $+0.011$ at two
answers) and then declines as answers accumulate, reaching $0.0991$ at eight answers---below the intercept. A
\emph{trained gated fold-to-point} operator fixes this by folding the answers into the latent itself and letting a
frozen decoder apply the full collaborative (intersection) structure:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cfold}
  \uu \;\leftarrow\; \uu + \sigma\!\bigl(g([\uu;\bphi_c;y_c])\bigr)\;\odot\;h([\uu;\bphi_c;y_c]),
\end{equation}
with the gate $g$ and the delta MLP $h$ \emph{zero-initialised} (so at initialisation $\uu$ is unchanged and R1
survives) and the accumulated shift norm-capped per user. In the concept-operator ablation it is monotone in the number of answers to eight
($0.1759/0.0841$ full/tail at eight answers, up from $0.1322/0.0503$ at one) and, crucially,
\emph{redundancy-robust}: feeding it eight \emph{correlated} top-SEL concepts still reaches $0.1685/0.0906$,
above its four-answer level ($0.1572/0.0777$), exactly where the additive-union operator degraded. The gated fold is the concept operator we adopt;
Eq.~\eqref{eq:cfold} is the concept-channel instantiation of the channel-agnostic fold of \S\ref{sec:method}.\footnote{This
operator ablation ran on the earlier tower. It settles the \emph{operator choice}---additive-union declines
where the gated fold stays monotone and redundancy-robust---rather than the absolute level; the deployed
numbers on the certified pair are in \S\ref{sec:concept:results}.}

\paragraph{Lineage.} The skeleton---an attribute as a latent direction, folded by a Bayesian belief update over a
frozen factor space---is well-trodden, and we claim none of it in isolation. B\i y\i k et al.~\cite{biyik2023soft} treat a
soft attribute as a concept-activation-vector direction and fold signed answers into a Gaussian belief; M\&Ms-VAE
folds a rating-posterior and an attribute-posterior over one latent by a product-of-experts
merge~\cite{antognini2021mmvae}; its successor adds an independent \emph{signed} critique axis through learned
positive/negative conditioning tokens, and reports that negative critiquing saturates after about five
turns~\cite{antognini2022posneg}---a concrete ceiling for any signed-attribute channel, consistent with our own
front-loaded concept curves. BK-VAE folds keyphrase-activation-vector directions by a Bayesian critiquing
step~\cite{yang2021bkvae}; the gate/MLP of Eq.~\eqref{eq:cfold} is a per-token generalisation of FiLM
conditioning~\cite{perez2018film}, which a single global affine cannot do because it would apply the same modulation to
item and concept tokens alike. And the decision to \emph{freeze} the tower rather than co-train a concept head is a
direct reading of the cautionary result that a bolt-on critique head costs recommendation
accuracy~\cite{wu2019dlc,luo2020cevae}.

\paragraph{The critiquing family looks channel-open and is not.} It is worth being precise here, because
critiquing is the one line that appears to accept free-form input and therefore appears to satisfy R2. It does
not: across the family the critique is defined as a \emph{one-hot indicator over a closed set}. DLC embeds a
one-hot keyphrase~\cite{wu2019dlc}; LLC states that critiques ``are encoded as one-hot keyphrase
indicators''~\cite{luo2020llc}; CE-VAE defines ``a one-hot vector with keyphrase length $s$''~\cite{luo2020cevae};
M\&Ms-VAE uses ``a one-hot vector of length $|K|$''~\cite{antognini2021mmvae}; Bot Play accumulates over a fixed
aspect set~\cite{li2021botplay}. The vocabularies are small---$40$ or $75$ keyphrases for catalogues of thousands
of items. Where the representation is continuous, the continuity is \emph{downstream of an argmax}. The lineage is
older than the neural work and has the same shape: FindMe's tweaks are fixed operations on a slot-value
schema~\cite{burke1996findme}, and Chen and Pu's survey defines a unit critique as one that ``can only constrain
over a single feature at a time''~\cite{chenpu2012critiquing}. The nearest counterexample is BCIE, whose critique
is a knowledge-graph fact drawn from $\sim$$57$k entities and $46$ relations~\cite{toroghi2023bcie}---three orders
of magnitude larger, and the right place to hedge R2's strength---but the fact must still be one the system
surfaced. What none of these holds jointly---and what we are aware of no system holding---is
the conjunction this channel occupies: \emph{continuous, out-of-catalogue concept directions} (a whitened centroid, or
a text-adapter image, with no per-attribute probe to train), driven by a \emph{signed, exposure-corrected implicit
affinity} (Eqs.~\eqref{eq:sel}--\eqref{eq:val}), folded over a \emph{frozen, certified full-profile frontier tower}. The
components are borrowed; only their conjunction is claimed.

\subsection{Deployment currency: why a capability gate is not enough}\label{sec:concept:fail}

A gate on what an operator \emph{can} express must be paired with a gate on what the deployed system
\emph{does} express, and the concept channel is where the distinction bites. The fold of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:cfold} is sign-capable: negating every \emph{concept} answer moves NDCG by $-0.2012$ with a
clean CI, so the concept channel passes R4's sign-flip test on its own operator. But that certifies the operator, not the interview. Driven down a
realistic, user-independent question bank, most concept answers are weak positives, and an answer model
that never emits a negative can accumulate them until the ranking drifts \emph{below} the no-answer
intercept while every capability gate still reads green.

We therefore evaluate the channel in \emph{deployment currency}: a fixed, user-independent bank under a
per-question budget where every question spends budget whether or not it is answered, scored at the
endpoint against the intercept (R3's fixed-bank setting). The acceptance form of the concept
channel is not ``can the operator express dislike'' but ``does the realizable interview stay above the
no-answer intercept at budget.'' This is the criterion the signed four-band estimator of
\S\ref{sec:concept:est} is built to satisfy, and \S\ref{sec:concept:results} reports it.

\subsection{Results: the signed retrain, and where the concept channel wins}\label{sec:concept:results}

The signed retrain of \S\ref{sec:concept:fail} has since landed, and we ran the two channels---concepts and
items---through \emph{one} deployment-currency harness (10{,}000 held-out cold users, per-question budget, every
question spending budget whether answered or not, scored on full \emph{and} tail NDCG@10 against the no-answer
intercept $0.1279/0.0192$). They tell a consistent story: the signed answer keeps the deployed interview
above the intercept, and which channel leads depends on how the question bank is ranked---so the comparison
is only meaningful once that ranking is stated, which is the finding this subsection builds to.

\paragraph{The signed answer turns the deployment decline into a gain.} Re-running the pre-registered triple gate on
the \emph{retrained} model (not merely the eval-only fold of \S\ref{sec:concept:fail}) clears all three taste
criteria. Signed answers beat the positive-only clip by $+0.0104$ full NDCG@10 at the sixteenth question (CI
$[+0.0075,+0.0134]$): the clip-up curve sinks below the intercept by the eighth question and stays there ($0.1268$ at
$q{=}16$), while the signed curve holds above it ($0.1372$ in this gate's arm). The counterfeit control---an answer built only from user
volume and concept popularity---reproduces \emph{none} of the gain (it declines, $-0.045$ at $q{=}16$), so the signal
is taste, not a volume$\times$popularity artifact; and permuting the answer values across concepts collapses the gain
($+0.0335$ CI-excluding-zero relative to the permutation), so it is the answer \emph{values} that carry it, not fold
cardinality. The two controls together establish what the channel is doing: the gain is carried by the answer
\emph{values}, and it is not a restatement of how much the user has watched.

\paragraph{Both channels carry an interview.} The instrument's claim on the elicitation side is deliberately modest,
and it is worth separating from the claim we do \emph{not} make. What the instrument chapter needs is that \emph{both}
channels fold and both move the ranking off the cold intercept---which is what makes the instrument usable for
measuring policies over a mixed question bank. That much is unambiguous: an interview conducted entirely in
out-of-catalogue concepts, a channel no R1-bar baseline can express at all, lifts a frontier-accuracy recommender
substantially and monotonically above its $0.1279/0.0192$ starting point, and so does an interview conducted entirely
in items.

What we deliberately do \emph{not} state here is a ranking between the two channels read off hand-picked
heuristics---a popularity-ordered item bank against a polarization-ordered concept bank, say---because such a
pairing measures the two heuristics at least as much as it measures the two channels. Constructing
\emph{optimal} question sequences per bank would answer it, but that is a selection result, and selection is
the companion chapter's subject rather than this one's. We therefore compare the channels the only way an
instrument paper can: hold the selection criterion fixed, apply it identically to both banks, and vary
nothing else.

\paragraph{Which channel wins depends on how the bank is ranked, and under one ranking the concept
channel wins outright.} The comparison above ranks each bank by a single criterion. That choice is not
neutral, and making it explicit produces the sharpest result in this section. We re-ran both channels
through one matched protocol---same cold users, same held-out targets, same budget, an unanswerable
question spending the budget in either arm, and at $m{=}0$ the two arms bit-identical by
construction---under four rankings of the same question banks: \textsc{pop} (most answerable),
\textsc{entropy} over the four-way ask outcome, \textsc{polar} $=H(\text{bands}\mid\text{answered})$
(pure discrimination), and \textsc{expinfo} $=P(\text{answered})\cdot H(\text{bands}\mid\text{answered})$.

Under \textsc{polar} the concept channel beats the item channel on \emph{both} metrics at \emph{every}
budget, every difference separated by a paired per-user bootstrap. At one question the gap is
$+0.0045$ full (CI $[+0.0017,+0.0073]$) and $+0.0109$ tail (CI $[+0.0062,+0.0159]$).
At eight it peaks at $+0.0189$ full (CI $[+0.0140,+0.0238]$) and $+0.0310$ tail
(CI $[+0.0243,+0.0374]$), and at sixteen it is $+0.0146$ full (CI $[+0.0094,+0.0197]$)
and $+0.0265$ tail (CI $[+0.0204,+0.0325]$). Under the other three rankings the
item channel wins full accuracy decisively: at $m{=}16$ under \textsc{pop} the gap is $-0.0782$
(CI $[-0.0857,-0.0707]$), and the tail difference is mostly not separated.

The mechanism is structural rather than a property of our fold, and it is measurable directly. A
maximally divisive \emph{item} is, by construction, one that few people have seen: the \textsc{polar}
item bank is answered $4.8\%$ of the time, so it forfeits almost every question and the item arm collapses
to $0.1352/0.0409$ at sixteen questions, barely above the $0.1277/0.0267$ no-answer intercept, while the
concept arm reaches $0.1498/0.0673$. A
maximally divisive \emph{concept} aggregates hundreds of films and stays answerable $97.2\%$ of the time
\emph{while still splitting people}. Concepts decouple discrimination from answerability; items cannot.
This is the R2 case in its strongest form: an instrument restricted to catalogue items cannot ask the
questions that separate users best, because those are exactly the questions its users cannot answer.

We are deliberately careful about what this does \emph{not} show. It does not show that concepts beat
items in general---off \textsc{polar} they lose full accuracy, clearly and separably. It shows that the
answer to ``which channel is better?'' is not a property of the channels alone, which is the same
structural point this chapter makes about the recommender in \S\ref{sec:instrdep}, now for the selection
criterion.

\paragraph{The concept channel's advantage is answerability, which is neither new nor surprising.}\label{sec:answerability}
That a cold user can answer a question about a broad property more readily than one about a particular
title is close to common sense: the concept question asks whether they like a kind of film, the item
question presupposes they have seen a specific one. We claim no discovery here, and the direction is
established independently of us. Whether a cold user \emph{can} answer at all is a first-class
concern of the classical interview literature, under other names. Rashid et al.\ select seed items by
popularity precisely because popularity is the probability that a new user has seen the
item~\cite{rashid2002getting}, and report that a pure-entropy strategy performs badly for exactly this
reason; Golbandi et al.'s tree splits users three ways, into lovers, haters and \emph{unknowns},
so unanswerability is a branch of the method rather than an
afterthought~\cite{golbandi2011adaptive}; and the active-learning survey of Elahi et al.\ gives the
property its own taxonomy category, as \emph{rateability} or acquisition
probability~\cite{elahi2016survey}. We adopt the term \emph{answerability} for readability, but we are
renaming a known quantity, not introducing one.

The term needs one qualification, because it is easy to read as more than it is. A user confronted with an
unseen film can always respond; what they cannot supply is a \emph{value}. Answerability here is the
probability that a question admits a graded response, not that it admits any response at all---Elahi et
al.'s \emph{rateability} is the more literal name for it. The distinction matters because the two consumers
that disagree most sharply in Table~\ref{tab:instrdep} treat the residual differently: a model that folds
observations gains nothing from ``I have not seen it'', whereas a tree that partitions users into lovers,
haters and unknowns is routed by precisely that response. Answerability is therefore not a property of a
question alone but of a question and the model reading its answer, which is the same
instrument-relativity this chapter documents for strategy orderings, one level down.

The sharpest prior statement of the problem is Sun et al.~\cite{sun2013learning}, by three of Functional
MF's own authors, whose entire contribution is an answerability fix: they report that a cold-start
interview tree becomes so unbalanced that \emph{the unknown branch captures more than $80\%$ of users at
each split}, so that users must repeatedly answer ``unknown'' before reaching a question they can engage
with, and may simply abandon the interview. Their remedy is to show several items per screen, which raises
the chance that a user knows at least one, and they evaluate against interview \emph{time} rather than
question count. That is the same pathology we measure, diagnosed twelve years earlier and more sharply.
Two things separate our result from theirs. Their fix stays inside the item channel---it makes item
questions more likely to land, rather than asking whether a different \emph{kind} of question lands more
often---and their multi-question screen is simultaneously more answerable \emph{and} more informative, a
confound they do not separate. Our comparison holds the budget and the interview protocol fixed and varies
only the channel.

The \emph{shape} of the curve is not new either. Gharahighehi et
al.~\cite{gharahighehi2025pairwise} run item-only against item-plus-genre trees and find that attributes
lead early while items overtake later; Xia et al.~\cite{xia2026when} find attributes dominating the
opening turns of a dialogue and items the later ones. Both report the crossover we report.

\paragraph{What we do claim, and how far it reaches.} Not that concepts are more answerable---the
literature above establishes that---but that on this instrument the quantity is \emph{visible}, so a
channel comparison can be read against it rather than inferred. The cleanest form of the measurement does
not involve a question bank at all. Take the hundred \emph{most popular} films in the catalogue---the most
answerable items that exist---and a held-out user has rated $25.2\%$ of them; widen to five hundred and it
falls to $13.1\%$. Against the whole $1{,}031$-concept vocabulary, unranked and unfiltered, the same users
can answer $53.0\%$. The gap is structural and unsurprising once stated: a concept is answerable if the
user has rated as few as two of its member films, so its answerability accrues from hundreds of chances,
where an item has exactly one. We are quantifying a property of \emph{generality}, not discovering an
advantage. What still depends on the setup is any per-budget count, which moves with the bank ranking and
the shortlist length, and the structural rule that decides when an item or concept counts as answerable;
the catalogue being films matters too. What generalises is the ordering and its cause, both of which predate us; what is ours is that
the instrument reports the number at all, and that the item channel turns out to face a trade-off the
concept channel does not---an item bank can be answerable or informative, and the two orderings differ.

\paragraph{On the adaptive headroom.} A one-level adaptive probe on the fixed stack---branch on the first answer, then choose the
second question per branch, selected and evaluated out-of-sample on disjoint halves---pays on the item channel with a
CI excluding zero ($+0.0015$ full, CI $[+0.0007,+0.0024]$; $+0.0016$ tail, CI $[+0.0008,+0.0024]$), and is positive
but not significant on the concept channel at one level. The effect is small because a one-level preview is not a
policy; that is the point. Establishing that adaptivity pays at all, on an instrument whose ingestion is certified, is
what this chapter owes the companion method chapter, whose job is to close the gap---the decision-tree acquisition
line of Golbandi et al.~\cite{golbandi2011adaptive} on \emph{this} instrument and harness. The instrument chapter's job
is to make the gap \emph{measurable}; closing it is the method chapter's.

\paragraph{The concept fold sits on the frozen tower, and inherits R1 by construction.} The signed concept fold is a
trained fold-to-point over the \emph{frozen} certified tower rather than a set of concept tokens trained into the
tower itself. That choice is what makes R1 free rather than re-earned: the fold's final layer is zero-initialised,
so at an empty interview it is bit-identical to the tower on the full profile, and no concept training can put the
certified full-profile number at risk. It also keeps the channel replaceable---a better concept operator can be
fitted and swapped without retraining or recertifying the recommender, which is the property that makes this an
instrument rather than a model.

\paragraph{The methodological consequence for verification design.} The concept channel's near-miss is the sharpest
argument in this chapter for \emph{why verification has the shape it does}. A capability check (R4's sign-flip) passed on
a fold that, as actually driven down a realistic question bank, never emitted a negative answer---the answer model, not
the operator, was sign-blind, and the operator's certified capability was vacuous in deployment. Only the
deployment-currency evaluation---a fixed, user-independent bank under a per-question budget, scored at the endpoint
(R3's fixed-bank setting)---exposed it, because there the declining curve penalises an answer model that
never exercises the negative half. This is a general failure class, not a concept-specific accident: \emph{a gate on
what an operator \emph{can} do must be paired with a gate on what the deployed system \emph{does}}. We record the
deployment-currency endpoint as the concept channel's acceptance form, and fold the signed-retrain numbers into
the R3/R4 rows of Table~\ref{tab:batteryresults}.

\subsection{Baseline bank}
The bank has three tiers, and each row of Table~\ref{tab:master} belongs to one of them. \emph{Classic floors}
that every curve must clear, which Dacrema et al.~\cite{dacrema2019progress} demand: Most-Popular as the
``did we learn anything'' line, Item-kNN as the Cremonesi-era strong-simple
baseline~\cite{cremonesi2010performance}, and PureSVD/iALS as cheap fold-in references. \emph{Full-profile
frontier}, which decides R1: EASE~\cite{steck2019ease} and RecVAE~\cite{ren2020recvae} as the bar,
Mult-VAE~\cite{liang2018variational} as the canonical amortised anchor, EDLAE~\cite{steck2020edlae} to
confirm the frontier is flat, plus the graph-filter and sequence families
(Turbo-CF~\cite{park2024turbocf}, SASRec~\cite{kang2018sasrec}). \emph{Elicitation-compatible rivals}, which
decide R3 and R4: EDDI~\cite{ma2019eddi} for the set-encoder-plus-info-gain architecture,
B\i y\i k~\cite{biyik2023soft} as the closest prior system, ConTS~\cite{li2021seamlessly} and
BCIE~\cite{toroghi2023bcie} for the conjugate-belief lineage, PEBOL~\cite{austin2024bayesian} as the
load-bearing preference-elicitation baseline, and Golbandi~\cite{golbandi2011adaptive} and
RBMF~\cite{liu2011wisdom,zhou2011functional} as the interview comparators.

We do not tabulate each system's published anchor. Those numbers are on the systems' \emph{own} splits and
metrics and are not comparable to ours until bridged, so printing them beside our ruler would invite exactly

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}
The protocol items below instantiate the certification bridge and the two outcome checks R1 and R4. The remaining
verification items and the two integrity items, which close the false positives those outcome checks admit, are
specified in Table~\ref{tab:batteryresults}, and each headline result is reported against them.

\paragraph{(i) The certification bridge.}\label{sec:bridge}
Our ML-25M ruler and the published bar are on \emph{different} metrics and splits, and we do not conflate them.
Concretely, our paper-config RecVAE realisation reaches full/tail NDCG@10 $0.3540/0.2497$ on the canonical ML-25M
split---whereas the published EASE/RecVAE numbers are NDCG@100 on the ML-20M strong-generalisation split. These are
not comparable, and no parity claim is made until the bridge is run. The bridge has two steps: (1) \emph{reproduce} the published EASE and Mult-VAE/RecVAE numbers on the ML-20M Liang
split (NDCG@100, Recall@20/50) in our own code, snapping to the reported values exactly~\cite{steck2019ease,liang2018variational,ren2020recvae};
(2) \emph{port} the certified tower to the ML-25M harness and report full/tail NDCG@10 there. We keep ML-20M in the
loop despite being older because the Liang 2018 split is a protocol lock-in the whole full-profile literature reports
on; ML-25M is the newer corpus and, crucially, carries the genome tags the concept channel needs, so both datasets are
required---one for comparability, one for the channels.

The first step is complete. On the ML-20M Liang strong-generalisation split our split statistics reproduce the
published counts exactly (9{,}990{,}682 events / 116{,}677 train users / 20{,}108 items), and both anchor
implementations \textbf{snap to their published values}: EASE at NDCG@100 $0.4203$ vs.\ the reported
$0.420$~\cite{steck2019ease}, and RecVAE at $0.4425$ vs.\ the reported $0.442$, with Recall@20 ($0.4144$ vs.\
$0.414$) and Recall@50 ($0.5525$ vs.\ $0.553$) matching as well~\cite{ren2020recvae}. One closed-form and one neural
reproduction to the third decimal certify that our split construction, metric implementation, and both
implementation families are faithful to the published protocol---every ML-25M baseline number below therefore
comes from certified code.

\textbf{Mult-VAE} reaches NDCG@100 $0.4223$ against the published $0.426$~\cite{liang2018variational}
($-0.0037$; Recall@20 $0.3928$ vs.\ $0.395$, Recall@50 $0.5341$ vs.\ $0.537$)---a \emph{near}-snap, close enough to
admit as a bridged Tier-2 anchor but not a third-decimal reproduction, and we label it that way rather than round it
into agreement. \textbf{Mult-DAE} \emph{fails}: $0.3969$ against the published $0.419$ ($-0.022$, with Recall@20 and
@50 short by a comparable margin). We do not know whether the gap is a training-recipe detail or something deeper, and
rather than carry an uncertified implementation we \textbf{drop Mult-DAE} from the baseline bank entirely---it earns no
row on the ML-25M ruler.

Consistency demands we apply that rule to ourselves, and doing so costs us something. Our iALS lands at $0.358$
against the published WMF anchor of $0.386$---a miss of $0.028$, \emph{larger} than the $0.022$ that disqualified
Mult-DAE. It would be a double standard to drop one and keep the other merely because the kept row is convenient, so
we state the rule and its consequence explicitly: \textbf{iALS is an uncertified floor, not a bridged anchor}. It
keeps its measured row on the ML-25M ruler, where it is one more number from the same harness, but it is excluded
from every claim that requires a certified comparator---no argument in this chapter rests on an unbridged number
being compared with another unbridged number. Only EASE, RecVAE and (as a near-snap) Mult-VAE carry certified
status on this ruler. The
bifurcation argument must stand on those or not at all.

\paragraph{(ii) R1 --- full-profile on ML-25M, items-only.}
On the baselines' native input (items only), evaluate full and tail NDCG@10 on the full ML-25M catalogue with a
paired per-user bootstrap CI, confirming the instrument's fold reproduces the frozen tower and is indistinguishable
from EASE/RecVAE on the tail, within a stated margin on the full profile. The item-only baselines are measured (Table~\ref{tab:master}, \textsc{liang} column).

\textbf{NDCG@100, the secondary metric}, orders the \textsc{liang} column identically and is reported here
rather than as a third pair of table columns: RecVAE $0.4537$, ours $0.4492$, EASE $0.4375$, EDLAE
$0.4330$, Mult-VAE $0.4303$, user-kNN $0.3958$, iALS $0.3700$, TaNP $0.3661$, RBMF $0.3533$, Turbo-CF
$0.3569$, Item-kNN $0.3200$, EDDI $0.3110$, the conjugate belief $0.2883$, SASRec $0.2465$, Most-Popular
$0.1975$. \textbf{Per-row configuration}, where a row required a choice: EDLAE at val-selected $p{=}0.4$;
Turbo-CF at val-tuned $\alpha{=}0.5$, $s{=}1$, second-order; EDDI early-stopped at epoch $46$ with epoch
$31$ val-selected; Mult-VAE a near-snap at $-0.004$ against its published anchor; TaNP best-effort with its
rating channel binarised, which is the row the \textsc{native} column still owes a graded retrain for; and
user-kNN, RBMF and the conjugate belief are best-effort cores of the elicitation-corner systems
(\S\ref{sec:related}) rather than their published systems.

\paragraph{A properly-trained RecVAE leads the closed-form frontier, and sets the bar.} On our canonical ML-25M
ruler our in-house paper-config RecVAE attains $0.3540$ full / $0.2497$ tail, the nominally strongest measured
system---ahead of the closed-form leaders EASE ($0.3476/0.2441$) and EDLAE ($0.3433/0.2395$). The top of this ruler
is a narrow band rather than a ranking: RecVAE leads EASE by $+0.0064$ full, about $1.8$ unpaired standard errors,
the same order as the tower's own $-0.0043$ separation from RecVAE below. We therefore
treat RecVAE as \emph{the bar}---the strongest measured system, and the conservative one to be held to---rather than
as a system separated from EASE. All rows share the same split, cohort, tail definition,
and NDCG@10 routine (verified by code inspection). The point of interest for the instrument is that on this ruler a
properly-trained amortised VAE---not a closed-form model---sits at the top of the frontier; the instrument's R1
obligation is precisely to \emph{stand on this bar}, reproducing RecVAE-class full/tail accuracy through the frozen
tower and then preserving it through the fold at R1. We report the whole frontier rather than excluding the stronger
baselines.

\paragraph{R1 is passed: the interview tower is indistinguishable from the bar on the tail at a $1.2\%$ full-profile cost, and is exactly the frozen decoder when the interview is
empty.} One certified training run of the graded-native set-encoder tower T2$'$---a single run of the pre-registered
winning configuration, selected on validation and scored once on the held-out test cohort---reaches full/tail NDCG@10
$\mathbf{0.3496/0.2491}$ against the RecVAE bar's $0.3540/0.2497$. Under a paired per-user bootstrap on the same
$10{,}000$ users, the tail difference is $-0.0006$ with CI $[-0.0020,+0.0008]$, which contains zero---indistinguishable in
the sense of Definition~\ref{def:tie}, i.e.\ a failure to separate rather than a demonstration of equivalence---while the full-profile difference is $-0.0043$ with CI $[-0.0059,-0.0028]$, which
\emph{excludes} zero. We therefore claim only that we cannot separate the two on the tail, alongside a bounded, separated shortfall of $1.2\%$ relative on
the full profile: interviewability is not free, and the price is small and stated rather than absorbed into a
significance test. Two properties of these numbers matter more than their size. First,
they are on the \emph{same} ruler as every other row of Table~\ref{tab:master}---same split, cohort, tail mask, and NDCG
routine---so the comparison is strict, not bridged. It is also matched on the same
\emph{information}: the tower reads the identical fold-in support as every other row (the level matrix is
built aligned to it, with an equal-\emph{nnz} assertion), differing only in that each item carries its
half-star level where the others carry a $1$. That difference is worth something, and we can say how much:
holding the support identical and replacing every star level with a single ``liked'' level costs
$0.3496\rightarrow0.3457$ full and $0.2491\rightarrow0.2471$ tail over all $10{,}000$ users, so the
intensity channel is worth $+0.0039$ full (paired CI $[+0.0025,+0.0053]$) and $+0.0020$ tail (paired CI $[+0.0007,+0.0031]$) at full profile---both intervals exclude zero. That number is worth reading
next to the gap to the bar. On the binary information RecVAE consumes, this tower would score $0.3457$,
some $-0.0083$ behind it; folding the half-star levels it already has recovers about half of that.
Interviewability costs the architecture something on the full profile, and the intensity channel buys back
roughly half the cost. The channel is \emph{most} valuable on the short interview, where the extra bits
arrive before collaborative signal can fill in (Figure~\ref{fig:gradedcurve}), but it is not free there and
worthless here.
Second, the R1 \emph{identity} half holds exactly and
not merely statistically: with an empty evidence set the tower emits a zero latent ($|\bmu_0|=0$ to numerical
precision) and its scores are a rank-identical image of the frozen decoder's learned bias (Spearman $1.0000$), which
is what licenses reading every later interview curve as evidence about \emph{ingestion} rather than about a shifted
baseline. The cold intercept of the interview curves in this chapter, $0.1279/0.0192$, is that same
non-personalised read-out; the zero-item concept curves of \S\ref{sec:concept:results} are scored under
their own protocol and carry their own intercept, $0.1277/0.0267$, which is why the two are never
compared across.
Accuracy is therefore settled for this instrument: R1 is evaluated through the same fold that every later
curve uses, so the full-profile standing reported here is a property of the ingestion path itself rather
than of a separate scoring route (\S\ref{sec:batteryresults}). Ranking here reads the folded point directly; whether an uncertainty estimate over
that point would help a policy \emph{choose} questions is the companion chapter's subject, not a claim of this one.

specific way of passing without the capability. R1 excludes a full-profile regression hidden in the
ingestion path. R2 excludes per-user retraining and a vocabulary fixed at fit time, and---because a concept
could be a popularity proxy rather than a direction---it also requires the fold to be
\emph{concept-specific}: a like lifts the concept's members to AUC $0.626$, a dislike pushes them to
$0.416$, and folding a \emph{different} answerable concept leaves them at chance ($0.498$). The raw
direction ranks its members below chance ($0.44$) and only reaches $0.94$ after whitening, which is what
makes a concept a direction at all; it beats the concept's own popularity projection by $+0.0087$.

controls are different objects, and we name them separately. The
lesson is that once a bank is restricted to answerable questions, \emph{which} of them is asked matters far
less than that they are answerable---which is precisely why the selection problem is deferred to the
companion chapter and why we decline to claim a selection result here.

\paragraph{(iii) R3 and R4 --- per-question curves.}
Cold-start per-question NDCG@10 curves (full and tail) versus a random-question control. Items are foldable by all
baselines; mixed channels (concepts, signed values, continuous tokens) are foldable only by our instrument, which is
the point of the comparison---the rivals cap out at what an item-only interview can carry. Symmetric access is
enforced where a baseline \emph{can} fold a channel; where it structurally cannot, that is reported as an architecture
limit, not hidden. The items-only R3 curves and the R4 sign and intensity arms are measured
(\S\ref{sec:batteryresults}), and the mixed-channel comparison is reported in \S\ref{sec:concept:results}
under one strategy-free criterion. No item-only baseline appears in that comparison, because none can fold
a concept at all---which is the architecture limit itself, argued in (iv) below.

\paragraph{(iv) Why ``just add genre columns'' is not an alternative.}
The obvious objection to the concept channel is that a concept could simply be appended to an item-only model as an
extra column---a centroid-of-members pseudo-item in EASE, or an extra input unit in RecVAE. The objection fails for a
structural reason rather than an empirical one, and the reason is worth stating precisely because it is the same
property that motivates the whole channel.

A column is fixed when the model is fitted. EASE's item--item weight matrix is defined over a fixed column set and is
obtained in closed form from the Gram matrix of exactly those columns; RecVAE's input layer has one weight vector per
column. Adding a concept therefore means adding a column, and adding a column means \emph{refitting the model}. This
is not a tuning inconvenience: it means the set of expressible concepts is frozen at training time. A paraphrase of an
existing concept, a concept invented after deployment, a direction produced by snapping a continuous query to the
nearest meaningful axis---none of these can be answered without retraining, because none of them has a column. The
pseudo-item is not merely a lossy encoding of a concept; it is a \emph{closed} one.

\subsection{Answer provenance: what may not enter an answer}\label{sec:firewall}

An interview evaluation is only as trustworthy as the thing that answers it, and the failure mode is specific: if
any quantity derived from the recommender reaches the answer, the interview curve measures the recommender
recovering its own geometry rather than ingesting evidence. That inflates every downstream number without
transferring to a real user, and it cannot be detected from the curve, because a circular answer model produces a
curve of exactly the shape a working one produces. We therefore treat the answer path as a protocol object in its
own right, with a rule stated in advance and enforced in code rather than checked afterwards.

\paragraph{The rule.} An answer may be computed only from the user's \emph{fold-in} behaviour and from
catalogue-level counts. Concretely: an item answer is the user's own recorded rating; a concept answer is the
signed log watch-lift of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sel}, formed from the user's watched members and a count expected from
their volume and the concept's catalogue popularity. Three things are excluded by construction, and the exclusion
is asserted rather than assumed:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{No recommender-derived quantity.} No latent, no factor, no score, no similarity and no
        model-predicted rating enters an answer at any point. The answer path never reads the tower.
  \item \textbf{No target-half information.} Answers are computed from the fold-in half only, so an answer cannot
        encode the held-out items it will be scored against.
  \item \textbf{No oracle over unobserved preference.} Where a user has no evidence about a concept, the estimator
        emits a \emph{refusal} rather than an imputed preference: an unexposed user is not assumed to hold a view.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Why this is worth stating as a criterion rather than a caveat.} Applied as a checklist, it separates
this literature sharply. PEBOL's simulated user answers from the ground-truth item
description~\cite{austin2024bayesian}---the answer is a function of the target. Unified item-and-attribute
conversational work assumes the user holds and reveals a clear preference over every attribute and every
item~\cite{deng2021unicorn}, which supplies an oracle exactly where a cold user has no evidence. Bandit-over-arms
formulations draw answers from the same latent space the recommender ranks in~\cite{li2021seamlessly}, which is
the circularity in its purest form. None of these is a careless choice; each is a reasonable simulator design. But
each makes the resulting interview curve a statement about the simulator as much as about the method, and none of
the three would pass the rule above.

We make no claim that our answers are what a \emph{person} would say---that is the scope boundary of
\S\ref{sec:intro} and the limitation of \S\ref{sec:limits}. The claim is narrower and checkable: whatever these
answers are, they are computed independently of the model being evaluated.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Full \emph{and} tail NDCG@10 on ML-25M (the project reporting rule, scored with the decoder's learned bias, not a
log-count popularity floor), plus NDCG@100 (and Recall@20/50) on the ML-20M bridge split for comparability with the
published anchors. Every headline delta carries a paired per-user bootstrap CI on one shared harness. Every cell reported is measured on this harness.

Five readings, and we are careful about which we claim.

\emph{The ranking is not a property of the heuristic alone.} Under our instrument \textsc{entropy0} leads on both
metrics; under the Golbandi node model \textsc{helf} leads on both; under a likes-only RecVAE
\textsc{popularity} leads on the full profile. Asked which heuristic is best, the three consumers give three
different answers, and each answer is separated. A paper reporting any one of them in isolation, with its own
recommender, would be reporting something true and non-transferable. This is the concrete sense in
which elicitation results are instrument-relative, and it is why we think a documented, certified, reusable
consumer is a contribution rather than plumbing.

\emph{What the consumers disagree about is what to do with a question nobody can answer.} The three
heuristics do not put equal numbers of graded answers in front of a model. Over the same $10{,}000$ users
at $k{=}8$, \textsc{helf} returns $1.59$ graded answers of eight, against $3.10$ for \textsc{entropy0} and
$3.05$ for \textsc{popularity}: a maximally divisive item is one few people have seen, so the strategy that
finds the most discriminative questions also finds the least answerable ones. A consumer that folds
observations is paid per graded answer, and \textsc{helf} therefore starves it---which is where the
likes-only RecVAE ($0.1003$) and RBMF ($0.0698$) sit. The Golbandi node model is paid differently: it
partitions users into lovers, haters and unknowns, so a question the user cannot answer still routes them,
and the same $1.59$-answer interview is the one it does best on ($0.1948$, its own strongest cell and the
strongest \textsc{helf} cell in the table). This is the instrument-relativity of answerability
(\S\ref{sec:answerability}) made concrete: the residual that is worthless to one consumer is the signal
another is built on. We claim no more than the account supports---\textsc{entropy0} and \textsc{popularity}
are within $0.05$ answers of each other and still change places between consumers, so answerability
explains why \textsc{helf} divides the table, not the remaining orderings.

\emph{This disagreement is not attributable to weak recommenders.} The likes-only RecVAE row is the control: it is the same frontier-accuracy model that sets the R1 bar, given the crudest interface an
item-only architecture admits, and it disagrees with our instrument \emph{and} with the Golbandi node model.
Whatever drives the disagreement, it is not that the other consumers are simply bad at recommending. The same row
carries a second reading. At full profile that model exceeds ours ($0.3540$ against $0.3496$); at an
eight-question interview it falls below ours by $+0.0293$ full and $+0.0258$ tail, each system on its own best
strategy, both separated. The ordering of the two systems therefore reverses between the two operating points.
We are careful about what that does and does not establish. It is not a controlled attribution: our tower is
initialised from that model but subsequently trained on interviews, so the difference confounds the input
contract with that training, and we do not separate them here. What it does establish is the weaker and
sufficient claim---that full-profile accuracy is not sufficient for interview accuracy, since the system with
the higher full-profile score is the lower-scoring interview consumer. That is the bifurcation of
\S\ref{sec:gap} observed within a single experiment rather than inferred across a literature.

\emph{The winning heuristic can depend on the metric as well as on the consumer.} For the likes-only RecVAE the
gap between \textsc{entropy0} and \textsc{popularity} is $-0.0042$ on the full profile and $+0.0042$ on the
tail. The two magnitudes coincide to four decimal places, which is a coincidence of rounding rather than a
shared decomposition: the full and tail metrics are computed over different candidate sets and different user
subsets ($10{,}000$ and $9{,}281$), so neither difference constrains the other. So even holding the consumer
fixed, ``which heuristic wins'' is not answered until the metric is named. We report full \emph{and} tail
throughout for exactly this reason.

\emph{We do not claim the heuristics are wrong, or that ours is the right consumer.} On the tail, \textsc{helf} is
first for two of the six consumers---the inversion is not a refutation of \textsc{helf}. Nor does our instrument
scoring highest in absolute terms settle which ordering an interview designer should trust: absolute accuracy and
faithfulness to the deployment recommender are different desiderata, and a designer whose production system is a
tree should arguably believe the tree. What the table forecloses is treating any single such ordering as a
property of the heuristics.

\emph{The instrument's own numbers are the strongest cells, which is the point of R1.} Our best cell exceeds the
Golbandi node model's best by $+0.0105$ on full and matches it exactly on tail ($.0792$ both). A consumer that were merely
different, rather than strong, would leave open the objection that the inversion is an artifact of a weak
recommender---which is exactly the objection Dacrema et al.~\cite{dacrema2019progress} showed to be usually
correct. R1 exists to close it.

\section{Limitations and threats to validity}\label{sec:limits}
% =====================================================================

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{One dataset, and the central claim is a general one.} Every number here is ML-25M under a
        single split recipe. The claim that a strategy ordering is a property of the strategy--recommender
        pair is demonstrated on six consumers, but on one catalogue, one rating culture and one interaction
        density; we have not shown that the \emph{same three} orderings arise elsewhere, only that a single
        ordering is not safe to report. The argument that a comparison must fix its consumer does not
        depend on the dataset, but the strength of the effect may; a replication on a second corpus is the
        first thing we would run next, and until it exists the quantitative inversion should be read as
        established for this ruler rather than for recommendation at large.
  \item \textbf{Metric non-comparability until the bridge is run.} Our frontier number $0.3540/0.2497$ is NDCG@10
        on ML-25M; the published bar is NDCG@100 on ML-20M. Until \S\ref{sec:bridge} snaps our code to the published
        numbers (done for EASE/RecVAE, \S\ref{sec:bridge}), \emph{no} parity claim across the two rulers is
        licensed---the ML-25M and ML-20M numbers live on different rulers.
  \item \textbf{Monotonicity is empirical, not a theorem.} Conjugate-Gaussian updates are monotone in expected
        utility~\cite{good1967principle,blackwell1953equivalent} \emph{only in expectation}, and only if the ranker acts optimally under the
        belief---which a fixed dot-product / NDCG ranker does not. R3, and the rising curve that checks it, is therefore a
        \emph{measured} property with a per-question CI, not a guarantee; a truthful answer can in principle still
        lower NDCG at a given step, and we report any such step rather than suppress it.
  \item \textbf{The protocol discards sub-threshold interactions.} The canonical recipe filters to
        $r>3.5$ before building its matrices, so $55.7\%$ of a test user's rated history---their
        dislikes---is absent from every model's input, ours included, and the candidate pool contains
        rated-and-disliked items that can never be targets. This is a property of the shared ruler rather
        than of any row on it: the discard is identical for every system, so comparative statements are
        unaffected, and our claim is one about standing on this protocol rather than an absolute one. It does,
        however, bear asymmetrically on R4, and we are precise about which half---and about what each
        measurement varies, since the two are easily confused. \emph{Intensity} within likes is measurable on
        this protocol: holding the fold-in support \emph{identical} and varying only the star level each item
        carries is worth $+0.0039$ full (paired CI $[+0.0025,+0.0053]$) and $+0.0020$ tail
        ($[+0.0007,+0.0031]$) over $10{,}000$ users (\S\ref{sec:expdesign}). \emph{Sign} is a different
        comparison---it varies \emph{what is folded}, adding the dislikes rather than re-valuing the likes---and
        is not measurable on this protocol at all, because a fold-in filtered to
        $r>3.5$ contains no dislike to fold: the protocol does not merely leave that capability unrewarded,
        it removes the input that would exercise it. What sign is worth at the full profile is therefore an
        open quantity on this ruler, and the evidence we report for it comes from the interview curves
        (Figure~\ref{fig:gradedcurve}), which are not governed by the full-profile protocol. We note also that
        masking the disliked items out of the candidate pool would \emph{not} be a repair: it would supply
        every model with a dislike-avoidance oracle the models themselves were denied, and in doing so
        remove precisely the items a dislike-aware recommender earns its advantage by demoting.
  \item \textbf{Answers come from recorded behaviour.} R3 and R4 are measured under the answer model that
        the answer-provenance rule fixes: an item answer is the user's own recorded rating and a concept answer is a log
        watch-lift over raw counts and item marginals, so no recommender-derived quantity can enter an answer and
        the self-simulator inversion is closed by construction rather than by measurement. These are real
        preferences, but they are not a person responding to a rendered question, and the chapter makes no claim
        about how a user would phrase, hedge or misread one.
  \item \textbf{BERT4Rec replicability.} The sequence baseline's fold-in advantage is subject to a documented
        replicability caveat~\cite{petrov2022replicability}; we tune it to the published protocol and flag any
        residual gap rather than treat a single run as authoritative.
  \item \textbf{Graph-filter admissibility.} GF-CF/BSPM/Turbo-CF~\cite{shen2021gfcf,choi2023bspm,park2024turbocf} do
        not publish an ML-20M strong-generalisation NDCG, so no row in this family can be a \emph{bridged} tie.
        Turbo-CF is re-run on our split with $(\alpha,s,\text{filter})$ selected on our validation users and is
        reported as best-effort tuned; GF-CF and BSPM are not run here and carry no number.
  \item \textbf{Scope of the novelty claim.} The conjunction claim is bounded by our survey: a set-encoder /
        deep-set recommender that reached RecVAE-class full-profile accuracy \emph{and} took value-carrying
        mixed-type tokens would partly pre-empt R1$\wedge$R2$\wedge$R4, and we found no such reported system.
\end{itemize}
