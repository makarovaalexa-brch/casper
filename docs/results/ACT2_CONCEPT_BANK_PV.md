## Verdict

**The action space was NOT the binding constraint.** Giving an adaptive selector the concept bank
does not let it convert personalisation value: of the 96 trend cells, **77 separate from zero and 75
of them are negative**; only 2 are positive, and both are an artefact addressed below. Under the
shuffle control **1 of 96** separates, which is what a 95% interval produces by chance on 96 cells.
The sign, the magnitude and the budget-dependence all match the parent table's item-bank battery —
if anything the concept askers are *worse*: at k=8 against `greedy_concepts` the Q4−Q1 trend runs
−0.038 to −0.080, where the item-bank selectors ran −0.021 to −0.035 against `greedy_items`.

**This is the design sheet's second reading**, and it triggers the retraction it specified: the
mechanism paragraph in `ACT2_PV_QUARTILES.md` — "the loss-optimal question is a *concept* for ~100%
of users at every budget while all three adaptive selectors shop in the item bank ... a concrete
hypothesis for *why* they cannot convert PV" — is **retracted in that document**, not quietly
dropped. The finding hardens to: **adaptive selection fails to convert personalisation value even
when the optimal action type is available to it, and fails harder.**

**Control 5 says the hypothesis really was tested, and where it was not.** Two arms ask concepts on
**100% of turns at every budget** — `conlinucb_bs_static`, which structurally cannot ask an item,
and `dpe_concept`, whose arm *is* the question — so no null here rides on an unfired schedule. Both
are negative against `entropy0` at every budget (bs_static −0.0090 / −0.0125 / −0.0461 / −0.0588
full at k=1/2/4/8; dpe_concept −0.0068 / −0.0074 / −0.0291 / −0.0438). The two ConLinUCB/ConUCB
schedule arms ask **0% of turns at k=1 and k=2** and **25% at k=4 and k=8** — b(t)=5⌊log t⌋ fires
only at turn 3 and turn 8 — so **their k=1 and k=2 rows are uninformative about concepts and must
not be read as a concept result**. That is exactly where the two positive cells sit:
`conlinucb_mcr − greedy_concepts` at k=2 (+0.0133 full, +0.0171 tail) is an arm that asked **zero**
concepts being compared against a concept order, i.e. an item-vs-concept comparison, not a
concept-asker converting PV. Where the schedule does fire, k=4 and k=8, the same comparison is
−0.0187 and −0.0375 full.

**The channel is not the problem — answerability is not the problem either.** Concepts are far more
answerable than items: the fixed concept order is answered on **90% / 89% / 90% / 79%** of turns at
k=1/2/4/8, against 44% / 43% / 41% / 39% for `entropy0` and 30% / 28% / 32% / 30% for
`greedy_items`. The concept bank delivers roughly three times the answers per turn, the loss-coupled
criterion says a concept is the optimal question for ~100% of users, and the adaptive selectors
still lose — and lose most where PV is highest.

**What the concept bank IS good for: a fixed order.** `fixedorder:greedy_concepts` is the strongest
arm in this table at every budget on both axes — 0.1783/0.0478, 0.1909/0.0548, 0.2147/0.0775,
0.2362/0.1022 at k=1/2/4/8 — beating `greedy_items` (0.2247/0.0776 at k=8) and `entropy0`
(0.2053/0.0792). Every concept-*asking* adaptive arm sits far below all three static orders
(best of them, `conlinucb_mcr`, 0.1902/0.0714 at k=8). So the concept channel is worth having; what
is not worth having is choosing within it adaptively.

**A caveat that cuts against calling this a clean adaptivity test, stated because it is measurable
rather than arguable.** `conlinucb_bs_static` conducts **1 distinct interview** over 10,000 users at
every budget — the barycentric spanner is user-independent by the paper's own construction, so this
arm is a *static concept order* chosen for spanning, not an adaptive selector. Its row is therefore a
**bank** result (spanner-optimal vs greedy-optimal vs entropy0), and it says the spanner is a bad
bank: −0.0540 full against `greedy_concepts` over all 10,000 users at k=8, with a refusal rate
climbing 0.129 → 0.581 as it works down to obscure concepts. `dpe_concept` does branch (10,000
distinct interviews from k=4) but its published initialisation makes that a seeded random
permutation of the bank, which its own module header states. The two arms that genuinely condition
the next concept on previous answers are `conlinucb_mcr` and `conucb`, at k=4 and k=8 only. Over the
24 trend cells that description covers (2 selectors × 3 comparators × 2 axes × 2 budgets),
**21 separate from zero, all 21 negative, none positive**, spanning −0.0111 to −0.0665; the 3 that do
not separate are all `conlinucb_mcr` at k=4 (+0.0015 full and −0.0013 tail vs `entropy0`, −0.0011
tail vs `greedy_items`), where its schedule has fired exactly once.

**A free cross-run control worth recording.** At k=1 and k=2, before its schedule fires,
`conlinucb_mcr`'s per-user NDCG vectors reproduce `glm_ucb`'s from the *parent* run at
**max\|delta\| = 0.0e+00** on both axes — the two arms are the same LinUCB index over the same
`GaussianBelief` while `M_t` carries no key-term row. At k=4 and k=8, where key-term rows enter,
they diverge (max\|delta\| 0.73–1.0). The only thing that changed is the concept turns, and the
identity breaks exactly where they appear. This is a test that varies the suspected cause, and it is
also a second cross-run 0.0e+00 alignment on an *adaptive* arm rather than a static one.

**What this does and does not license.** It does not show that no concept bandit could convert PV —
