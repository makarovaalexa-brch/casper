**SO PHASE B = PORT THE PROVEN RECIPE ONTO THE SET ENCODER.** The genuinely untested parts are only:
(a) u1 is the DENSE architecture (fixed input slot per concept) — dead for continuous queries, which is why the
    author killed it;
(b) **u1's concept answers were ORACLE (member-mean), NOT the realistic answerer** — this is the honest gap;
(c) the set encoder has never seen a concept token.
**Given the mechanism we just measured (the winning questions are NICHE and GENRE-CONGRUENT), the concept
channel should be STRONGER, not weaker:** "do you like Japanese animation?" is the same discriminator as
*Laputa*, but **answerable by far more people** (you need not have SEEN Laputa) and higher-bandwidth.

**Build:**
1. Extend the set-encoder's embedding table: **+1,128 genome-tag rows, +500 entity/attribute rows** (bank
   layout: concepts[0:1128], entities[1128:1628], items[1628:2428] — already aligned 1:1 to `ConceptBank`).
2. **Warm-start** the new rows from pop-weighted member-bags (the encoder-faithful init in `ConceptBank`).
3. **FREEZE THE DECODER** (author's original staging). The decoder is the SOTA scorer; concepts must learn to
   SPEAK ITS LANGUAGE, not reshape it. Keep a subset-distillation anchor so full-profile strength does not rot.
4. Token fusion for concepts = the SAME FiLM form (`gamma[level] * emb + beta[level]`), but the level is the
   ANSWERER's ordinal (hated/meh/liked/loved) — **value, confidence and refusal stay SEPARATE learned signals,
   never multiplied.**

**Gates (pre-registered):**
- **G-strength:** full-profile NDCG@10 must NOT drop below the Phase-A base (0.4852).
- **G-concept-cold:** concept-only cold-start must BEAT the popularity intercept (the additive operator failed

## STAGE 2b — CONTINUOUS QUERIES + SNAP + RERANK (author: "add continuous to that list")
**In the set encoder a continuous query is FREE: it is just ANOTHER TOKEN** — a d-dim vector instead of an
embedding-table lookup. Same trunk, same frozen decoder, no new machinery. **This is the payoff the whole
architecture was built for, and the dense model structurally could not give it.**
- **Emit** `q` in the shared latent space (items ∪ concepts ∪ arbitrary directions live in ONE space).
- **SNAP + RERANK (proper Wolpertinger — we currently do NOT do this).** Our snap is **TOP-1 argmax cosine with
  NO critic re-rank** (`continuous_actor.py:187`) — **the exact configuration Wolpertinger reports as FAILING**
  on a 13k-action recommender ("actions with low Q-value may occasionally sit closest to q̂"). Correct form:
  retrieve the **k nearest askable phrases**, then **RE-RANK THEM WITH A VALUE HEAD** and ask the winner.
- **SNAP-K sweep (the honesty test):** `k in {1, 5, 20, 60, 120, 250, |bank|}` — k=1 is our current setting,
  k=|bank| is a full re-rank (the ceiling). **This is a SWEEP, not a cap.**
  - Snap-loss COLLAPSES under k-NN + re-rank => part of Paper C's centrepiece was a RETRIEVAL ARTIFACT. Say so.
  - Snap-loss PERSISTS => the continuity prize is about directions **no phrase names** — the strong thesis, now
    DEFENDED against the obvious referee attack.
- **Also worth trying (2026 lit, "Breaking the Grid"):** train the actor toward a **Q-softmax-weighted BLEND of
  the retrieved candidates** rather than the nearest one. All gradients come from EXECUTABLE, EVALUATED phrases,
  and the regression target is generically OFF-CATALOG (a blend of phrases is no phrase) — so it TEACHES the
  actor to land between phrases. **It is the same object as our Paper-E OMP sparse-blend renderer: the mechanism
  we use to SPEAK the query is the mechanism we should use to TRAIN it. "Ask the blend, not the nearest phrase."**
- **Do NOT add a snap-consistency/commitment penalty** (`lam*||q - snap(q)||^2`). Our NN snap into a real phrase
  bank CANNOT fail (every point maps to a valid askable question) — unlike HyAR/VQ, whose learned decoders can.
  Such a penalty would optimise away the very quantity we claim is valuable. Run it ONLY as a pre-registered
  lam-sweep showing a NON-MONOTONE curve with an INTERIOR optimum (precedent: PLAS, CoRL 2020).

## STAGE 3 — THE POLICY, AT SCALE (what the author asked for: "do this at scale with a good policy model")
The probe used the crudest conditioning imaginable (an 18-way genre cut) and got +47%. A real policy conditions
on the **belief state**, not a bucket.
- **Architecture** (converged on independently by our own notes AND the amortized-BOED literature): a
  **Set-Transformer trunk over the exchangeable (question, answer) history** — no positional encoding, since
  the order you asked in does not matter — plus a **belief-conditioned POINTER HEAD** that scores all 2,428
  askable candidates. At 2,428 actions a full softmax is free; Wolpertinger's kNN trick is for 10^5-10^6.
- **Per-candidate belief features** (closed-form, cheap): `mu^T q` (what we think they'd say),
  `q^T Sigma q` (how unsure we are in that direction), `1/2 log(1 + q^T Sigma q / sigma^2)` (exact one-step
  EIG), popularity, learned answerability, already-asked mask.
- **TRAIN ON THE TASK LOSS, NOT ON EIG-ABOUT-z.** This is the one lesson everything agrees on: Sepliarskaia
  (RecSys 2018) — a static questionnaire BEATS adaptive decision trees precisely because the adaptive method
  optimised a variance surrogate; TNDP (NeurIPS 2024) — design for Decision Utility Gain. **Our probe ranked by
  NDCG and the order became user-specific. Ranking by entropy would likely collapse it to one list.**
- **Trainer:** supervised distillation from the probe's own per-cluster/per-belief argmax FIRST (it already
  works, and DPT gives it a theory), THEN value-based fine-tune. **Never bare REINFORCE** (eight failures).
- **Start with the frozen-trunk variant**: freeze the recommender encoder, train ONLY the pointer head. It
  trains in minutes, cannot destabilise the recommender, and tests "was the actor the bottleneck?" with one
  variable changed.

  not a curriculum we impose.**
